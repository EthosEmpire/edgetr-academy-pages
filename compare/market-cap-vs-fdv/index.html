The idea in one picture

Both figures multiply a price by a number of units. They differ in which units they count. Market cap counts what is circulating now. Fully diluted valuation, or FDV, counts everything that is ever planned to exist.

In plain words

A bakery has sold 100 loaves this morning at 2 each, and plans to bake 1,000 in total.

“Market cap” is 100 x 2 = 200: the loaves that are out there, at today’s price.

“FDV” is 1,000 x 2 = 2,000: what all the loaves would come to if every one of them sold at 2. Whether loaf number 900 still fetches 2 is a guess, since nobody has tried to sell it yet.

How each is calculated

Market cap = price x circulating supply
FDV = price x maximum supplyWhere no maximum is defined, total supply is commonly used instead.

The supply terms are explained in token supply. Circulating supply is the number of units in public hands. Maximum supply is the most that can ever exist under the asset’s rules. Units that are locked, reserved for a team or investors, or not yet issued are counted in FDV and not in market cap.

When the distinction matters

FDV rests on a hypothetical

FDV applies today’s price to units that are not for sale today. That price was set by trading in the circulating supply alone. If the remaining units enter the market, the balance of supply and demand changes, and nothing ensures the price stays where it was. FDV is a useful measure of how much supply is outstanding. It is not a valuation anyone has paid.

Dilution

A large gap between the two figures means that most of the supply has yet to arrive. It arrives through token unlocks and emissions, on a schedule that is usually published.

The same token after more supply arrivesHypothetical illustration

A hypothetical token trades at 2. Circulating supply is 100 million and maximum supply is 1,000 million.

  • Market cap: 100M x 2 = 200M.
  • FDV: 1,000M x 2 = 2,000M.
  • Share of supply circulating: 100M / 1,000M = 10%.

A year later, circulating supply has grown to 400 million. Two of many possible situations:

  • The market cap is unchanged at 200M. The price is then 200M / 400M = 0.50. Each unit has fallen 75% while the total value of circulating units stayed the same.
  • The price is unchanged at 2. The market cap is then 400M x 2 = 800M. For that to be true, buyers have absorbed 600M of additional value.

Neither is a forecast. The arithmetic shows that when supply grows, price and market cap cannot both stay still.

Comparing assets

Two assets with the same market cap can have very different FDVs. One may have nearly all its supply circulating, the other a small fraction. Comparing them on market cap alone hides that difference, and comparing them on FDV alone treats locked supply as if it were already trading. For an asset with no maximum supply, an FDV based on total supply also leaves out future issuance.

Common confusion

  • Market cap is not money invested. It is the last price multiplied by a unit count. A small trade at a higher price raises the market cap of every unit at once. See market capitalization.
  • Market cap is not what the supply could be sold for. Selling a large share would move the price, especially where liquidity is thin.
  • Circulating supply is not measured precisely. Data sites use their own methods and can report different figures for the same asset.

What it does NOT mean

  • A low market cap does not mean “room to grow”. It describes present size and carries no information about the future.
  • A high FDV does not mean the price must fall. It indicates that more supply is scheduled. The effect depends on demand at the time.
  • FDV is not a target or a fair value. It is one multiplication with a strong assumption inside it.
  • Neither figure measures quality, safety or usefulness. They are measures of size, and of how much supply is yet to come.

Key takeaway

Market cap prices the supply that circulates today, and FDV prices all the supply that will ever exist as if today’s price applied to it. The gap between them shows how much supply is still to come, and FDV’s assumption about price is a hypothetical.
