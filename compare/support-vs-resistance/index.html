The idea in one picture

Support and resistance are the same idea seen from two sides. Each is an area on the chart where price has turned before. The only difference is where it lies relative to the current price: support is underneath, resistance is overhead.

In plain words

Think of a ball bouncing in a room. The floor is support and the ceiling is resistance. Both are surfaces the ball has bounced off before.

Now picture a multi-storey building. If the ball breaks through the ceiling into the room above, that ceiling is now the floor beneath it. The surface has not moved. The ball is on the other side.

Why these areas form

The conventional explanation is about memory and unfinished business. At a price where a decline previously turned, some participants bought and were pleased with the result, some meant to buy and missed it, and some sold and regretted it. If price returns there, all three groups have a reason to buy. The mirror image applies at a price where an advance turned.

This is a description of how people commonly behave, not a mechanism that has to operate. The fuller explanation is in support and resistance basics.

When the distinction matters

The label depends on where price is

Nothing about a level makes it permanently one or the other. A level is support while price is above it and resistance while price is below it.

When price moves decisively through a resistance area and later returns to it from above, the area often acts as support. This is called role reversal. It is common enough to have a name and unreliable enough to fail regularly.

One level, two namesHypothetical illustration

A hypothetical asset has turned up from the area around 100 three times and turned down from the area around 110 three times. Price is now 104.

  • The area near 100 is support: it is below price, and declines have ended there.
  • The area near 110 is resistance: it is above price, and advances have ended there.

Price then rises through 110 and trades at 114. A week later it falls back to 110.50 and turns up again.

The area near 110 is now being described as support. Its price has not changed. What changed is that the market is trading above it, so the same people’s decisions now show up as buying on the way down, where before they showed up as selling on the way up.

Had price fallen from 114 straight back through 110 to 107, the move above 110 would be described as a fakeout, and 110 would be called resistance once more.

What a break is called

A move up through resistance is a breakout. A move down through support is a breakdown. They are mirror images, and both can fail.

Areas, not lines

Price rarely turns at exactly the same number twice. Support and resistance are better drawn as bands with some width, a point developed in zones, not lines. A level drawn as a thin line invites the mistaken conclusion that a move of a few cents beyond it means something.

Common confusion

  • Support is not only for buyers and resistance only for sellers. Every participant sees both. The words describe what price has done, not who may act.
  • A level is not stronger simply because it is older or was touched more often. Some practitioners read many touches as strength, others as a level being worn down. Neither reading is a rule.
  • Levels are not objective. Two people looking at the same chart will often draw different levels, and different timeframes show different ones.
  • Moving averages and trend lines are also called support and resistance. These slope and move with time, and are known as dynamic support and resistance.

What it does NOT mean

  • Support does not mean price cannot fall further. It marks where declines have ended before. Every breakdown starts at a support level.
  • Resistance does not mean price cannot rise further. Every breakout starts at a resistance level.
  • Price at support is not “cheap”, and price at resistance is not “expensive”. The words describe position on a chart, not value.
  • A bounce does not confirm the level for next time. Each visit is a separate event with an unknown outcome.
  • Neither is a signal. They are reference areas for describing where price is and what would count as a change.

Key takeaway

Support is an area below price where declines have tended to stop, and resistance is an area above price where advances have tended to stop. They are the same phenomenon on opposite sides of the market, they can exchange roles, and neither is a barrier.
