Compare: clear up the confusing pairs

Long or short? Coin or token? EMA or SMA? Each page puts two easily confused concepts next to each other, then explains when the difference actually matters.

  • LongvsShortLong and short describe the direction of a position: a long gains if price rises, a short gains if price falls.
  • BullishvsBearishBullish means expecting or describing rising prices, bearish means expecting or describing falling prices. Both are views or conditions, not positions.
  • SpotvsPerpetualsSpot trading means buying and owning the asset itself. A perpetual is a leveraged contract that tracks the asset's price, with funding payments and the risk of liquidation.
  • CEXvsDEXA centralized exchange (CEX) is a company that holds customer assets and matches trades. A decentralized exchange (DEX) is a set of smart contracts that users trade against from their own wallets.
  • CoinvsTokenA coin is the native asset of its own blockchain. A token is an asset issued by a smart contract on a blockchain that already exists.
  • Hot walletvsCold walletA hot wallet keeps its keys on a device connected to the internet. A cold wallet keeps them offline. The trade-off is between convenience and exposure to remote attack.
  • CustodialvsNon-custodialWith a custodial wallet, a third party holds the private keys on the user's behalf. With a non-custodial wallet, the user holds the keys and carries the full responsibility.
  • Proof of WorkvsProof of StakeProof of Work secures a blockchain with computation and energy. Proof of Stake secures it with locked-up coins. Both make it costly to rewrite the ledger.
  • Market ordervsLimit orderA market order trades immediately at the best prices available. A limit order trades only at a chosen price or better, and may not trade at all.
  • EMAvsSMABoth are moving averages of past prices. An EMA gives recent prices more weight and reacts faster than an SMA of the same length, at the cost of more noise.
  • RSIvsMACDRSI is a bounded momentum oscillator that moves between 0 and 100. MACD is an unbounded trend-momentum indicator built from two exponential moving averages.
  • SupportvsResistanceSupport is a price area below the market where declines have tended to stop. Resistance is a price area above the market where advances have tended to stop.
  • Market capvsFDVMarket cap is price times the supply circulating today. Fully diluted valuation (FDV) is price times the maximum supply, which assumes today's price would hold for every future unit.
  • Isolated marginvsCross marginIsolated margin normally limits the loss on a position to the margin assigned to it. Cross margin backs every position with the shared account balance, so one position can draw on all of it.
  • TradingvsInvestingTrading seeks to gain from price moves over shorter periods. Investing holds assets for years on a view of long-term value. They differ in horizon, method, costs and workload.
  • APRvsAPYAPR is a yearly rate without compounding. APY is the yearly rate with compounding included. For the same underlying rate, APY is the higher number.
  • Stop lossvsLiquidationA stop loss is the trader's own planned exit order. Liquidation is a forced close by the exchange when margin no longer covers the loss. One is chosen, the other is imposed.
  • Layer 1vsLayer 2A Layer 1 is a base blockchain with its own consensus. A Layer 2 processes transactions off that base chain and settles the results back to it, aiming for lower fees and higher capacity.

Learn the market. Understand the tools. Make your own decisions.
