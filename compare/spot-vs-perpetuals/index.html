The idea in one picture

On a spot market, a trade is an exchange. One asset is handed over, another is received, and the buyer owns what was bought. On a perpetual futures market, nothing is exchanged except a promise: a contract whose value rises and falls with the asset’s price. The trader holds a position, not the asset.

In plain words

Buying spot is like buying a house. It is yours, and if its value falls you still have a house, although its value can fall a long way.

A perpetual is like a bet with a deposit on what the house will be worth. If the value moves against you far enough, the deposit is taken and the bet is over, even if the value recovers the next day.

How a perpetual stays near the spot price

A perpetual never expires, so there is no settlement date to pull its price toward the spot price. That job is done by the funding rate: a payment exchanged between longs and shorts at regular intervals, often every eight hours, though the interval differs between exchanges.

When the contract trades above the spot price, longs typically pay shorts. When it trades below, shorts typically pay longs. The payment makes the crowded side more expensive to hold.

When the distinction matters

Leverage changes the size of every outcome

A perpetual position is opened by posting margin, a fraction of the position’s value. Leverage is the ratio between the two. It multiplies gains and losses by the same factor, measured against the margin.

The same 10% fall, three waysHypothetical illustration

A trader has 1,000. The asset is priced at 100 and then falls 10% to 90. All numbers are hypothetical, and fees and funding are ignored.

  • Spot. 1,000 buys 10 units. At 90 they are worth 900. Loss: 100, or 10% of the 1,000. The 10 units are still owned.
  • Perpetual at 5x. 1,000 of margin supports a position of 5,000, which is 50 units. Loss: 50 x 10 = 500, or 50% of the margin.
  • Perpetual at 10x. 1,000 of margin supports a position of 10,000, which is 100 units. Loss: 100 x 10 = 1,000, or 100% of the margin. In practice the position is liquidated before price reaches 90, because exchanges require a minimum margin to be maintained. On some venues, a fast move can leave a loss larger than the margin.

The price move is identical in all three cases. If price later returns to 100, the spot holder is back where they started. The liquidated position no longer exists.

Liquidation is a leveraged-position risk

A spot holding bought without borrowing cannot be closed by the exchange because the price fell. Spot bought with borrowed funds, or pledged as collateral, can be sold in a liquidation. A leveraged position can be closed in part or in full. When losses reduce the margin below a required minimum, the exchange closes part or all of the position. This is liquidation, and the rules for it differ between venues.

Time has a cost on one side

Spot has no ongoing charge for holding. A perpetual position pays or receives funding for as long as it is open. Over days and weeks this can become a meaningful cost, and its size and direction change with market conditions.

Common confusion

  • A perpetual is not “the coin with leverage”. No coin is bought. The position cannot be withdrawn, and it exists only on the venue where it was opened.
  • Funding is not usually a fee to the exchange. On most exchanges it passes between traders. It can be received as well as paid. It can be received as well as paid.
  • The perpetual price is not always the spot price. The two track closely and can differ, especially in fast markets. Exchanges use a separate mark price to decide liquidations.

What it does NOT mean

  • Spot does not mean safe. A spot asset can lose most or all of its value, and assets held on an exchange carry the exchange’s own risks.
  • Perpetuals do not mean more gain. Leverage enlarges results in both directions, and adds funding costs and liquidation.
  • Neither market is the “real” one. Both are widely used, for different purposes, and regulators’ advisories describe leveraged derivatives as high risk for retail participants.
  • Availability does not mean permission. Access to leveraged derivatives is restricted in some jurisdictions.

Key takeaway

Spot is ownership of the asset. A perpetual is a leveraged contract on its price, with funding payments and liquidation. The same price move can leave a spot holder with a smaller holding and a leveraged trader with nothing.
