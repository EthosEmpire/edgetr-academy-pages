The idea in one picture

A moving average replaces a jagged series of prices with a smoother line. The SMA and the EMA are the two most common versions. With the same length setting, they are built from similar data and differ in how they weight it. The EMA stays closer to price and the SMA trails further behind.

In plain words

Two teachers grade a student over ten tests. The first averages all ten equally. The second counts the latest tests more, because they show where the student is now.

After one surprising result, the second teacher’s grade moves further. It responds sooner to real improvement, and it also overreacts more to a single unusual day.

How each is calculated

SMA = (sum of the last n closes) / nEach close in the window has a weight of 1/n.
EMA today = close x k + EMA yesterday x (1 - k), where k = 2 / (n + 1)For n = 10, k is about 0.18, so the newest close carries about 18% of the weight.

In a 10-period SMA, the newest close has a weight of 10%. In a 10-period EMA it has about 18%, and each older close counts for progressively less.

One jump, then calmHypothetical illustration

A hypothetical asset closes at 100 for many periods, so a 5-period SMA and a 5-period EMA both read 100. For the EMA, k = 2 / (5 + 1) = 1/3.

The next close is 106.

  • SMA: (100 + 100 + 100 + 100 + 106) / 5 = 506 / 5 = 101.20.
  • EMA: 106 x 1/3 + 100 x 2/3 = 35.33 + 66.67 = 102.00.

The EMA moved by 2.00 and the SMA by 1.20.

The closes then return to 100 and stay there.

  • The SMA remains at 101.20 for the four periods in which 106 is still inside its window. On the fifth it drops straight back to 100.00, although nothing happened in the market that day.
  • The EMA steps down gradually: 101.33, then 100.89, 100.59, 100.40, and so on, approaching 100 without a sudden move.

The EMA reacted more to the jump. The SMA reacted twice: once when the price entered the window and again when it left.

When the distinction matters

Speed against noise

Because the EMA follows price more closely, it turns earlier when a move begins. For the same reason it turns more often when nothing lasting is happening. The SMA filters out more of the small movements and is later at every real turn. This is one trade-off seen from two sides, and no setting escapes it.

The length matters more than the type

A 20-period EMA and a 20-period SMA are much more alike than a 20-period and a 200-period average of either type. A shorter length makes any average faster and noisier. Choosing between EMA and SMA is a fine adjustment by comparison.

Crossovers

When two averages are compared, as in moving average crossovers, the EMA versions cross earlier and cross more often. In a sideways market that means more crossings that lead nowhere.

Levels that many people watch

Some averages are followed widely enough to act as reference points, such as the 50 and 200 period averages on daily charts. Which type is meant depends on the community and the tool, so two charts can show the “200-day average” at slightly different prices.

Common confusion

  • Faster is not more accurate. The EMA is quicker to follow price. It is not better at describing what comes next.
  • Both lag. Neither average can turn before price does. See lagging vs leading indicators.
  • The EMA does have a memory. Its starting value affects early readings, so the first stretch of an EMA on a chart can differ between tools.
  • There are other types. Weighted, smoothed and volume-weighted averages exist. EMA and SMA are simply the most common.

What it does NOT mean

  • Price above an average does not mean it will keep rising. It means the current price is higher than the average of recent prices.
  • Neither average is a level price must respect. Prices pass through moving averages constantly.
  • A common setting is not a tested one. Lengths such as 20, 50 and 200 are conventions, not optimised values.
  • Choosing EMA or SMA does not create an advantage. Both restate the same past prices with different weights.

Key takeaway

An EMA weights recent prices more and follows price closely, and an SMA weights all prices in its window equally and is smoother. The EMA buys speed with noise, the SMA buys smoothness with delay, and both describe the past.
