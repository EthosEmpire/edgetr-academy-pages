APR

Annual Percentage Rate: the rate per period multiplied up to a year. It assumes payouts are not reinvested.

vs

APY

Annual Percentage Yield: the yearly result if every payout is reinvested at the same rate. It includes interest earned on interest.

Both are covered in depth on one page

The idea in one picture

Both figures express a rate of return or cost over one year. The difference is compounding: earning a return on earlier returns. APR leaves it out. APY builds it in. The same product can therefore be advertised with two different percentages, both correct.

In plain words

You are paid 1 apple a month for every 100 apples you store.

If you eat each month’s apple, you collect 12 in a year. That is 12%, the APR.

If you add each apple to the pile, next month’s payment is on 101 apples, then on a little more. After a year you have gained slightly more than 12. That is the APY.

How each is calculated

APR = rate per period x number of periods in a year
APY = (1 + r/n)^n - 1r is the APR as a decimal and n is the number of compounding periods in a year.

12% a year, paid monthlyHypothetical illustration

A hypothetical deposit of 1,000 pays 1% a month.

APR. 1% x 12 = 12%. If each month’s payout of 10 is withdrawn, the year brings 12 x 10 = 120. End value: 1,120.

APY. If each payout is added to the deposit, the balance is multiplied by 1.01 twelve times. 1,000 x 1.01^12 = about 1,126.83. The gain is 126.83, so the APY is about 12.68%.

The difference is 6.83 on 1,000. Both figures describe the same 1% a month, and both assume that 1% is paid in every one of the twelve months.

When the distinction matters

The gap grows with the rate

At modest rates, APR and APY are close. At high rates they separate sharply. An APR of 100%, compounded daily, corresponds to an APY of about 171%. A platform showing “171%” and another showing “100%” may be describing the identical rate.

Comparing two offers

Two percentages can only be compared when they are the same kind. An APR next to an APY is not a like-for-like comparison, and an APY is only meaningful together with its compounding frequency.

Compounding has to actually happen

APY assumes that every payout is reinvested, at once, at the same rate. Where reinvesting requires a separate transaction, each one may cost a network fee, and on small amounts the fee can exceed the extra return. An APY that cannot be achieved in practice overstates the result.

The rate is a snapshot

In traditional savings products a quoted rate is often fixed for a term. In crypto, rates on staking, lending and liquidity pools are usually variable and can change from one block to the next. A displayed figure is typically a recent rate projected over a full year, on the assumption that nothing changes. It is not a promise.

What the payout is made of

Many crypto yields are paid in a token. A rate of 20% in a token that loses half its value is not a 20% return in money terms. The percentage describes a quantity of units, and the worth of those units is a separate matter.

Common confusion

  • APY is not a better deal than APR. It is a different way of stating a rate. For the same product, the APY figure is simply the larger of the two.
  • APR has a narrower legal meaning in consumer lending. In some countries, a loan’s APR must include certain fees. In crypto the terms are used loosely, and platforms differ in what they include.
  • Borrowing is quoted the same way. For a borrower, the compounding in APY works against them: it is the cost that grows.
  • A yearly figure does not mean a yearly commitment. The number is annualised for comparison, whatever the actual holding period.

What it does NOT mean

  • A quoted APY does not mean that return will be received. It is a projection from a current rate that can change, and very high figures often fall quickly.
  • A high rate does not mean a good opportunity. Unusually high yields generally reflect higher risk, newly issued tokens or temporary incentives.
  • Neither figure accounts for risk. Smart contract failures, platform insolvency, loss of the principal and falls in the token’s price lie outside the percentage.
  • Yield is not income without a source. Every rate is paid by someone or by new issuance, and understanding where it comes from is part of understanding the number.

Key takeaway

APR is a yearly rate with no compounding, and APY is the same rate with compounding included, calculated as (1 + r/n)^n - 1. Quoted crypto yields of either kind are variable projections, not guarantees, and say nothing about the risks behind them.
