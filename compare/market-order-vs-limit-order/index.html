The idea in one picture

Every order answers two questions: at what price, and when. No order type controls both. A market order fixes the timing and accepts whatever price is available. A limit order fixes the price and accepts that the trade may happen later or never.

In plain words

A market order is walking into a shop and saying “I’ll take it, whatever it costs today”. You leave with the item.

A limit order is leaving a note that says “I’ll buy it if the price is 99 or less”. You may get a call tomorrow, next month, or not at all.

How each one meets the order book

An exchange keeps an order book: a list of buy orders (bids) and sell orders (asks) waiting at different prices.

A market order is matched at once against the waiting orders on the other side, starting with the best price and moving outward until it is filled. A limit order that cannot be matched straight away joins the book and waits its turn at its price.

Buying 12 units, two waysHypothetical illustration

Using the hypothetical book above, a trader wants to buy 12 units.

With a market order, the order takes the cheapest asks first:

  • 5 units at 100.10 = 500.50
  • 5 units at 100.20 = 501.00
  • 2 units at 100.40 = 200.80

Total paid: 1,202.30 for 12 units, an average of about 100.19. The screen showed a best ask of 100.10. The difference of about 0.09 per unit is slippage.

With a limit order to buy at 100.10, only the 5 units offered at that price are bought, for 500.50. The remaining 7 units wait in the book as a bid at 100.10. They fill only if someone later sells at that price. If the market moves up and away, they never fill.

One order was completed at a worse average price. The other got the stated price on part of the size and nothing on the rest.

When the distinction matters

Thin and fast markets

In a deep market, a small market order fills at or very near the displayed price. In a market with little liquidity, or during a sudden move, the same order can reach far into the book and fill at prices well away from the one on screen.

Fees

Most exchanges charge two rates. An order that removes liquidity pays the taker fee. An order that rests in the book and is later matched pays the maker fee, which is usually lower. Market orders are always takers. The rates and tiers differ between venues and are covered under trading fees.

Urgency against price

When the purpose of an order is to be out of a position immediately, an unfilled limit order has failed at its job. When the purpose is to trade at a particular price or not at all, a market order has no way of expressing that. The two types serve different priorities.

The spread

A market buy pays the ask and a market sell receives the bid. The gap between them, the spread, is a cost of immediacy on every market order. A resting limit order does not pay it, and waits instead.

Common confusion

  • A limit order can behave like a market order. A buy limit placed above the current ask is matched immediately, up to its limit price. It is then a taker.
  • “Or better” is part of the definition. A buy limit at 100 can fill at 99.90 if that is available. It cannot fill at 100.10.
  • A stop order is a different thing. A stop order waits for a trigger price and then usually becomes a market order, with the same uncertainty about the fill.
  • The last traded price is not the price of the next trade. A market order fills against the book, not against the number on the chart.
  • A partial fill is a normal outcome. A limit order can be filled in pieces over time, or only in part.

What it does NOT mean

  • A market order does not mean “the market price”. There is no single market price. There is a bid, an ask and a queue of sizes behind each.
  • A limit order does not mean a better result. It can miss a move entirely, and the missed trade does not appear in any record.
  • A lower fee does not make a resting order cheaper overall. Waiting carries its own cost when the price moves away.
  • Neither type is the correct default. Each gives up one kind of certainty to obtain the other.

Key takeaway

A market order controls when a trade happens and leaves the price open. A limit order controls the price and leaves the timing open, including the possibility of no trade. The choice between them is a choice of which uncertainty to accept.
