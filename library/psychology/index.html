Trading Psychology

FOMO, FUD, biases and the habits that damage decision-making.

Start here: Why Psychology Matters

14 pages

Why Psychology Matters Trading psychology is the study of how emotions and mental shortcuts shape decisions about money, and why knowing a rule is different from following it. Beginner
FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) FOMO is the anxious urge to join a price move because others appear to be profiting from it, usually after much of the move has already happened. Beginner
FUD (Fear, Uncertainty & Doubt) FUD is negative information, accurate or not, that spreads fear about an asset or market. The word is also used to wave away criticism that may be valid. Beginner
Fear & Greed Fear and greed are the two emotions most often used to describe market behaviour: greed pushes people toward risk as prices rise, fear pushes them away as prices fall. Beginner
Revenge Trading Revenge trading is taking impulsive, often larger trades straight after a loss in an attempt to win the money back quickly. Beginner
Overtrading Overtrading is trading more often, in larger size or in more markets than a person's own plan and capital support, usually driven by emotion and not opportunity. Beginner
Loss Aversion Loss aversion is the tendency for a loss to hurt more than a gain of the same size pleases, which leads people to treat losing and winning positions differently. Intermediate
Confirmation Bias Confirmation bias is the tendency to look for, notice and remember information that supports what one already believes, and to discount what contradicts it. Intermediate
Anchoring Anchoring is the tendency to rely too heavily on one reference number, such as a purchase price or a past high, when judging what a price is worth now. Intermediate
Recency Bias Recency bias is the tendency to give the most recent events too much weight and to expect that what has just happened is what happens next. Intermediate
Herd Behavior Herd behavior is the tendency of people to copy what a large group is doing, buying because others buy and selling because others sell, instead of judging independently. Intermediate
Overconfidence Overconfidence is the tendency to overrate one's own skill, knowledge or control over outcomes, which in trading leads to larger positions and thinner safety margins. Intermediate
Sunk Cost Fallacy The sunk cost fallacy is continuing with a decision because of the money, time or effort already spent on it, even though those costs cannot be recovered. Intermediate
Patience & Discipline Patience is waiting until the conditions of a plan are met. Discipline is doing what the plan says once they are, including the parts that are uncomfortable. Beginner
