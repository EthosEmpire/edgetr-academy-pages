Break of Structure

A break of structure is the moment price moves beyond a swing point that defined the trend. In an uptrend it is commonly defined as a close below the most recent higher low.

Why it matters

A trend is defined by its swing points, so it follows that a trend can be un-defined by them too. A break of structure is the event that does this: price moves beyond the swing point that was holding the current description together.

The idea is useful because it is specific. It replaces “the trend feels weak” with a price and a condition that either has or has not been met. It is also widely misunderstood as a signal, which it is not.

What it looks like

The most common definition is:

  • in an uptrend, a break of structure is price closing below the most recent higher low;
  • in a downtrend, it is price closing above the most recent lower high.

In the figure, the uptrend made a higher low at 116 and then a higher high at 130. As long as price stays above 116, the staircase of higher lows is intact. The close below 116 interrupts it.

In plain words

Imagine climbing a ladder where each rung is higher than the last. A break of structure is the moment you step down below the rung you were standing on a moment ago. You have not fallen off the ladder. You have, for the first time, stopped climbing.

How to read it

A break of structure answers one narrow question: does the old description of the market still fit? After a break in an uptrend, the answer is “not cleanly”. It does not answer what comes next. Three outcomes are common:

After the break What it looks like Structural result
Reversal A bounce makes a lower high, then price makes a lower low A downtrend is now defined
Range Price moves sideways around the broken level Neither trend is defined
Failed break Price quickly returns above the broken low and goes on to a new high The uptrend resumes. See fakeouts.

A break is one stage in the longer process described in trend transitions. On its own it shows that one condition of the trend has failed.

Definitions differ

There is no governing body for chart vocabulary, and this term is used in more than one way.

Wick or close. Some traders count any trade beyond the swing point. Many require a candle to close beyond it, on the reasoning that a close shows the market accepting the new price. The close-based rule filters out some brief spikes at the cost of reacting later.

Which swing point. A chart has large swings and small swings nested inside them. A break of a small internal swing low is a much weaker event than a break of the major low that anchors the whole trend. Readers who disagree about which swing matters reach different conclusions from the same chart. See swing highs and swing lows.

Which direction the term refers to. In some trading communities, “break of structure” means a break in the direction of the existing trend, such as a new higher high in an uptrend, and a different name is used for the first break against the trend, commonly “change of character” or “market structure shift”. When reading other material, check which meaning the author intends.

Timeframe. A break on a five-minute chart happens many times a day and often reflects an ordinary pullback on a larger chart. See multi-timeframe structure.

Going deeper

A classical idea with a modern name. Dow Theory treats a trend as intact until price moves below a prior reaction low (or above a prior rally high). “Break of structure” is newer vocabulary for a closely related observation.

Structure and levels overlap. A higher low is also a small support area, so a break of structure in an uptrend is at the same time a breakdown. The broken low is often watched afterwards as possible resistance, following the logic of role reversal.

The same candles under two rulesHypothetical illustration

A hypothetical uptrend has a higher low at 116 and a higher high at 130. Price then falls.

  • Candle A trades down to 115.4 and closes at 116.8.
  • Candle B closes at 117.5.
  • Candle C closes at 114.

Under a wick-based rule, the break happened on candle A, because price traded below 116. Under a close-based rule, candle A was not a break, since it closed above 116, and the break happened on candle C.

Two readers of the same chart would report the break at different times. After candle C, price bounces to 114.5, still below 116, and then falls to 108: a lower high and a lower low. Had price instead closed back above 116 and risen past 130, the same break would be remembered as a failed one.

What it does NOT mean

  • A break of structure is not a reversal. It shows that the trend’s definition has been interrupted. A reversal needs further swings in the new direction.
  • It is not a buy or sell signal. It is an observation about swing points, and many breaks fail.
  • It does not have one agreed definition. Wick or close, major or minor swing, and even the direction the term refers to all vary between sources.
  • A break on a small timeframe does not change the larger trend. Each timeframe has its own structure.

Common mistakes

  1. Treating every break as the end of the trend. Ranges and failed breaks are frequent outcomes.
  2. Using minor swings to judge a major trend. Small internal lows break constantly inside healthy trends.
  3. Deciding mid-candle. Under a close-based rule, a candle that is below the level with time remaining has not yet broken anything.
  4. Switching rules to suit a view. Using wicks when a break is wanted and closes when it is not removes the value of having a rule.

Risks and limitations

  • The event is identified only after price has already moved away from the high or low.
  • Brief moves beyond a swing point that reverse are common, particularly around obvious levels where many orders rest.
  • Because definitions vary, statements about a “break of structure” are only meaningful when the rule being used is stated.

Key takeaway

A break of structure is price moving beyond the swing point that defined the trend, commonly a close below the most recent higher low in an uptrend. It marks the point where the old description stops fitting, and it does not say what replaces it.
