Candlesticks

Single, double and multi-candle patterns: anatomy, context and failure cases.

Start here: Candle Anatomy Bullish and bearish gallery

29 pages

Candle Anatomy A candle packs four prices from one period, the open, high, low and close, into a single shape made of a body and two wicks. Beginner
Candlestick Context & Confirmation A candle pattern's shape matters less than where it forms, what came before it, the timeframe, the volume and how the next candle closes. Beginner
Doji A Doji is a candle whose open and close are almost the same price. It shows that neither buyers nor sellers won the period. Beginner◆ Neutral
Dragonfly Doji A Dragonfly Doji opens and closes at or near its high and has a long lower wick. Sellers pushed price down and buyers recovered all of it. Beginner▲ Bullish context
Gravestone Doji A Gravestone Doji opens and closes at or near its low and has a long upper wick. Buyers pushed price up and sellers took all of it back. Beginner▼ Bearish context
Long-Legged Doji A Long-Legged Doji has long wicks on both sides and an open and close that are almost equal. Price swung widely and ended where it began. Beginner◆ Neutral
Hammer A hammer is a candle with a small body at the top of its range and a long lower wick, forming after a decline. It shows that lower prices were rejected. Beginner▲ Bullish context
Inverted Hammer An inverted hammer is a candle with a small body at the bottom of its range and a long upper wick, forming after a decline. It depends heavily on confirmation. Beginner▲ Bullish context
Hanging Man A hanging man is a hammer-shaped candle, a small body on top of a long lower wick, that forms after an advance. It is read as a caution, not a conclusion. Beginner▼ Bearish context
Shooting Star A shooting star is a candle with a small body at the bottom of its range and a long upper wick, forming after an advance. It shows that higher prices were rejected. Beginner▼ Bearish context
Spinning Top A spinning top is a candle with a small body and wicks on both sides. Price moved in both directions and finished only slightly away from where it started. Beginner◆ Neutral
Marubozu A Marubozu is a candle that is all body, with little or no wick at either end. One side controlled the period from the open to the close. Beginner
Wicks & Rejection A wick shows prices that traded during a period but did not hold until the close. A long wick is often described as the market rejecting those prices. Beginner
Bullish Engulfing A bullish engulfing pattern is two candles after a decline: a bearish candle followed by a bullish candle whose body covers the whole of the first body. Beginner▲ Bullish context
Bearish Engulfing A bearish engulfing pattern is two candles after an advance: a bullish candle followed by a bearish candle whose body covers the whole of the first body. Beginner▼ Bearish context
Bullish Harami A bullish harami is two candles after a decline: a long bearish candle, then a small candle whose body sits inside the first body. It shows selling pressure pausing. Intermediate▲ Bullish context
Bearish Harami A bearish harami is two candles after an advance: a long bullish candle, then a small candle whose body sits inside the first body. It shows buying pressure pausing. Intermediate▼ Bearish context
Piercing Line A piercing line is two candles after a decline: a bearish candle, then a bullish candle that opens lower and closes above the midpoint of the first body without covering it. Intermediate▲ Bullish context
Dark Cloud Cover Dark cloud cover is two candles after an advance: a bullish candle, then a bearish candle that opens higher and closes below the midpoint of the first body without covering it. Intermediate▼ Bearish context
Tweezer Top & Bottom A tweezer top is two neighbouring candles with matching highs after an advance. A tweezer bottom is two with matching lows after a decline. Each shows a price tested twice and not passed. Intermediate
Inside Bar & Outside Bar An inside bar is a candle whose whole range sits within the previous candle's range. An outside bar exceeds the previous range on both sides. One shows contraction, the other expansion. Intermediate
Morning Star A morning star is three candles after a decline: a long bearish candle, a small-bodied candle, then a long bullish candle that closes well into the first body. Intermediate▲ Bullish context
Evening Star An evening star is three candles after an advance: a long bullish candle, a small-bodied candle, then a long bearish candle that closes well into the first body. Intermediate▼ Bearish context
Three White Soldiers Three white soldiers are three long bullish candles in a row, each opening within the previous body and closing higher, near its high. They show steady buying over three periods. Intermediate▲ Bullish context
Three Black Crows Three black crows are three long bearish candles in a row, each opening within the previous body and closing lower, near its low. They show steady selling over three periods. Intermediate▼ Bearish context
Three Inside Up & Down Three inside up is a bullish harami followed by a third candle that closes higher. Three inside down is a bearish harami followed by a lower close. Each is a harami plus one candle of follow-through. Advanced
Three Outside Up & Down Three outside up is a bullish engulfing pattern followed by a third candle that closes higher. Three outside down is a bearish engulfing followed by a lower close. Advanced
Rising & Falling Three Methods Rising three methods is a long bullish candle, several small candles inside its range, then another long bullish candle closing higher. Falling three methods is the mirror: a pause inside a trend. Advanced
Gaps A gap is an empty space on a chart where no trades took place, because price jumped from one level to another between two candles. Gaps are common in stocks and rare in 24-hour crypto markets. Intermediate
