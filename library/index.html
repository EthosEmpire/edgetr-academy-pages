Candlesticks

Single, double and multi-candle patterns: anatomy, context and failure cases.

All 29 pages
Candle Anatomy A candle packs four prices from one period, the open, high, low and close, into a single shape made of a body and two wicks. Beginner Candlestick Context & Confirmation A candle pattern's shape matters less than where it forms, what came before it, the timeframe, the volume and how the next candle closes. Beginner Doji A Doji is a candle whose open and close are almost the same price. It shows that neither buyers nor sellers won the period. Beginner◆ Neutral Dragonfly Doji A Dragonfly Doji opens and closes at or near its high and has a long lower wick. Sellers pushed price down and buyers recovered all of it. Beginner▲ Bullish context

Chart Patterns

Triangles, flags, wedges, channels, tops and bottoms, explained by structure and invalidation.

All 20 pages
How to Read Chart Patterns A chart pattern is a named summary of how price behaved across several swing points. Patterns describe structure; they are subjective, probabilistic and much clearer in hindsight. Beginner Double Top A double top is two peaks at a similar height, separated by a pullback, that form after an advance. It is conventionally read as a possible reversal if the low between the peaks breaks. Beginner▼ Bearish context Double Bottom A double bottom is two lows at a similar depth, separated by a bounce, that form after a decline. It is conventionally read as a possible reversal if the high between the lows breaks. Beginner▲ Bullish context Triple Top & Triple Bottom A triple top is three peaks at a similar height; a triple bottom is three lows at a similar depth. Each is conventionally read as a possible reversal once the opposite side of the range breaks. Intermediate

Indicators

What each line measures, how it is calculated, common settings and what it cannot tell you.

All 26 pages
How Indicators Work A technical indicator is a calculation applied to past price and volume data, drawn on the chart to make one aspect of market behaviour easier to see. Beginner Moving Averages A moving average is a line that shows the average price over a set number of recent candles, smoothing out short-term noise so the general direction is easier to see. Beginner SMA (Simple Moving Average) The simple moving average is the plain arithmetic mean of the last N closing prices, with every close in the window given exactly the same weight. Beginner EMA (Exponential Moving Average) The exponential moving average is a moving average that gives the most weight to the newest closing prices, so it reacts to changes sooner than a simple average. Beginner

Market Structure

Swings, trends, ranges and the transitions between them.

All 10 pages
What Is Market Structure? Market structure is the pattern of swing highs and swing lows that price leaves behind. It describes whether a market is trending up, trending down or moving sideways. Beginner Swing Highs & Swing Lows A swing high is a peak with lower highs on both sides, and a swing low is a trough with higher lows on both sides. They are the turning points used to read market structure. Beginner Uptrend An uptrend is a sequence of higher swing highs and higher swing lows. Price climbs in steps, and each pullback ends above the low of the one before it. Beginner▲ Bullish context Downtrend A downtrend is a sequence of lower swing highs and lower swing lows. Price falls in steps, and each bounce ends below the high of the one before it. Beginner▼ Bearish context

Support & Resistance

Levels, zones, breakouts, retests, fakeouts and confluence.

All 12 pages
Support & Resistance Basics Support and resistance are price areas where buying or selling interest has repeatedly appeared. Support sits below price, resistance sits above, and neither is an exact line or a guarantee. Beginner Support Support is a price area below the market where buying interest has repeatedly been strong enough to pause or reverse a decline. It may hold again, and it may not. Beginner Resistance Resistance is a price area above the market where selling interest has repeatedly been strong enough to pause or reverse an advance. It may hold again, and it may not. Beginner Zones, Not Lines Support and resistance are better drawn as zones, bands of price, than as single lines, because markets rarely turn at exactly the same price twice. Beginner

Bull, Bear, Long & Short

Four words beginners mix up: market conditions versus position direction.

All 9 pages
Bull, Bear, Long & Short Explained Bull and bear describe a market or an opinion about it, while long and short describe a position someone holds. Keeping the two pairs apart removes a very common beginner confusion. Beginner Bull Market & Bullish A bull market is a sustained period of generally rising prices, and bullish describes an upward condition or interpretation. Both are descriptions, not instructions to hold a long. Beginner▲ Bullish context Bear Market & Bearish A bear market is a sustained period of generally falling prices, and bearish describes a downward condition or interpretation. Both are descriptions, not instructions to open a short. Beginner▼ Bearish context Long Position A long position is one that gains value if the price rises and loses value if it falls. It can be as simple as owning an asset, or it can be a leveraged contract with very different risks. Beginner▲ Bullish context

Trading Mechanics

Orders, spot and perpetuals, leverage, margin, funding, liquidation and P&L.

All 19 pages
How a Trade Works A trade starts as an order, becomes a fill when it is matched with another trader's order, lives as an open position, and ends when that position is closed. Beginner Order Book An order book is the live list of all waiting buy and sell orders for one market, sorted by price, from which the exchange matches trades. Beginner Bid, Ask & Spread The bid is the highest price a buyer is offering, the ask is the lowest price a seller will accept, and the spread is the gap between the two. Beginner Order Types An order type is the set of conditions attached to an order: whether it trades immediately, only at a chosen price, or only after a trigger price is reached. Beginner

Risk Management

Position sizing, stop placement, risk/reward, drawdown and survival.

All 13 pages
What Is Risk Management? Risk management is the set of decisions, made before the outcome is known, that limit how much a single trade or a whole account can lose. Beginner Risk Per Trade Risk per trade is the amount an account would lose if one trade is closed at its planned stop, usually expressed as a percentage of the account. Beginner Position Sizing Position sizing is the calculation of how large a trade can be so that the loss at the planned stop equals the amount chosen as risk per trade. Beginner Risk/Reward Ratio The risk/reward ratio compares the planned loss on a trade, from entry to stop, with the planned gain, from entry to target. Beginner

Trading Psychology

FOMO, FUD, biases and the habits that damage decision-making.

All 14 pages
Why Psychology Matters Trading psychology is the study of how emotions and mental shortcuts shape decisions about money, and why knowing a rule is different from following it. Beginner FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) FOMO is the anxious urge to join a price move because others appear to be profiting from it, usually after much of the move has already happened. Beginner FUD (Fear, Uncertainty & Doubt) FUD is negative information, accurate or not, that spreads fear about an asset or market. The word is also used to wave away criticism that may be valid. Beginner Fear & Greed Fear and greed are the two emotions most often used to describe market behaviour: greed pushes people toward risk as prices rise, fear pushes them away as prices fall. Beginner

Trading Styles

Scalping, day, swing and position trading, and the approaches used within them.

All 13 pages
Overview of Trading Styles A trading style describes how long positions are usually held and what kind of price behaviour an approach is built around. No single style suits everyone. Beginner Trading and Investing Trading and investing both mean buying and selling assets. They differ in time horizon, in what a decision is based on, and in the costs and attention they demand. Beginner Scalping Scalping is a style built on very short holding periods, from seconds to a few minutes, and very small price moves. It is highly sensitive to fees, spread and execution. Intermediate Day Trading Day trading is a style in which positions are opened and closed within the same day, so nothing is held overnight. It demands hours of attention and many trades. Beginner

TradingView & Chart Tools

Chart layouts, timeframes, drawing tools, scales, alerts and indicator settings.

All 11 pages
Chart Interface Basics A charting screen is built from a few standard parts: a price pane, a time axis, a price axis, symbol and timeframe selectors, drawing tools, indicator panes and a watchlist. Beginner Chart Types: Line, Bar, Candle & More Chart types are different ways of drawing the same price data. Line, bar, candlestick, Heikin Ashi, area and Renko charts each keep some information and leave some out. Beginner Timeframes on a Chart A chart's timeframe sets how much time each candle covers. Changing it regroups the same trades into different candles, which changes the picture and every indicator value. Beginner Price Scales: Log vs Linear A linear price scale gives equal space to equal price changes, while a logarithmic scale gives equal space to equal percentage changes. The same data can look very different on each. Intermediate
