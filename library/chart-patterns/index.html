Chart Patterns

Triangles, flags, wedges, channels, tops and bottoms, explained by structure and invalidation.

Start here: How to Read Chart Patterns Bullish and bearish gallery

20 pages

How to Read Chart Patterns A chart pattern is a named summary of how price behaved across several swing points. Patterns describe structure; they are subjective, probabilistic and much clearer in hindsight. Beginner
Double Top A double top is two peaks at a similar height, separated by a pullback, that form after an advance. It is conventionally read as a possible reversal if the low between the peaks breaks. Beginner▼ Bearish context
Double Bottom A double bottom is two lows at a similar depth, separated by a bounce, that form after a decline. It is conventionally read as a possible reversal if the high between the lows breaks. Beginner▲ Bullish context
Triple Top & Triple Bottom A triple top is three peaks at a similar height; a triple bottom is three lows at a similar depth. Each is conventionally read as a possible reversal once the opposite side of the range breaks. Intermediate
Head and Shoulders A head and shoulders is three peaks after an advance, with the middle one highest, and a neckline under the two lows between them. It is conventionally read as a possible reversal if the neckline breaks. Intermediate▼ Bearish context
Inverse Head and Shoulders An inverse head and shoulders is three lows after a decline, with the middle one deepest, and a neckline over the two highs between them. It is conventionally read as a possible reversal if the neckline breaks. Intermediate▲ Bullish context
Rounded Top & Rounded Bottom A rounded top or bottom is a slow, curved change of direction with no sharp turning point. It describes a trend that fades gradually, and it is usually clear only after it has finished. Intermediate
Bull Flag A bull flag is a sharp rise followed by a short, tight drift downward between two roughly parallel lines. It is conventionally read as a pause in an advance, and it often fails. Beginner▲ Bullish context
Bear Flag A bear flag is a sharp fall followed by a short, tight drift upward between two roughly parallel lines. It is conventionally read as a pause in a decline, and it often resolves the other way. Beginner▼ Bearish context
Pennant A pennant is a sharp move followed by a brief pause in which highs fall and lows rise into a small triangle. It is conventionally read as a continuation pattern, in either direction. Intermediate
Rectangle A rectangle is a sideways pause in which price moves between two roughly horizontal boundaries. It describes balance between buyers and sellers and can resolve in either direction. Beginner
Ascending Triangle An ascending triangle is a flat line of highs above a rising line of lows. It shows buyers paying more on each dip while sellers hold one level, and is conventionally read as bullish. Beginner▲ Bullish context
Descending Triangle A descending triangle is a flat line of lows beneath a falling line of highs. It shows sellers accepting less on each rally while buyers hold one level, and is conventionally read as bearish. Beginner▼ Bearish context
Symmetrical Triangle A symmetrical triangle is a series of lower highs and higher lows that converge toward a point. It shows a market in shrinking balance and has no built-in direction. Beginner
Rising Wedge A rising wedge is price climbing between two upward-sloping lines that converge. Each new high gains less ground than the last, which is conventionally read as weakening momentum. Intermediate▼ Bearish context
Falling Wedge A falling wedge is price declining between two downward-sloping lines that converge. Each new low gains less ground than the last, which is conventionally read as fading selling pressure. Intermediate▲ Bullish context
Channels A channel is a trend contained between two roughly parallel lines: a trend line on one side and a parallel channel line on the other. It describes the rhythm of a trend, not its future. Beginner
Cup and Handle A cup and handle is a rounded dip and recovery to a prior high, followed by a smaller pullback. It is conventionally read as a pause within an advance, completed if price closes above the rim. Intermediate▲ Bullish context
Broadening Formation A broadening formation is a series of higher highs and lower lows, so the swings grow and the boundary lines spread apart. It describes expanding volatility with neither side in control. Advanced
Pattern Failure & Invalidation Chart patterns fail often. Invalidation is the condition, stated in advance, under which a pattern reading no longer fits the chart, and a failed pattern is information about the market, not an error. Intermediate
