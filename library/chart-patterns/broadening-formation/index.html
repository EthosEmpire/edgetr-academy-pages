Broadening Formation

A broadening formation is a series of higher highs and lower lows, so the swings grow and the boundary lines spread apart. It describes expanding volatility with neither side in control.

Why it matters

Most patterns on this shelf describe a market that is becoming more orderly: ranges tighten, swings shrink, a level gets tested. The broadening formation describes the opposite. Each swing goes further than the last in both directions, and the chart becomes harder to read as it develops.

It is worth knowing for two reasons. It is a clear picture of rising volatility and disagreement. It also explains a frustrating experience: a market in which every move beyond a previous high or low quickly reverses.

What it looks like

The conventional recognition rules are:

  • at least two highs, each higher than the last;
  • at least two lows, each lower than the last, alternating with the highs;
  • an upper line that rises and a lower line that falls, so the two spread apart;
  • swings that grow in size as the pattern develops.

The shape is also called a megaphone or an expanding triangle.

How it forms

In plain words

Think of a child on a swing being pushed harder each time. Every arc goes higher than the last, forward and back. The swing is clearly gaining energy. Nothing about the arcs tells you on which side it ends up when the pushing stops.

In structural terms the market is making higher highs and lower lows at once, which fits neither an uptrend nor a downtrend. Each new high draws in buyers who see an advance, and then fails. Each new low draws in sellers who see a decline, and then fails. The classic texts describe this as a market without a settled view, driven by strong and shifting emotion.

How to read it

Element Conventional reading What is subjective
Higher highs Buyers briefly in control Which highs the line goes through
Lower lows Sellers briefly in control Which lows the line goes through
Diverging lines Expanding range Lines rarely fit the swing points well

Volume. Classic descriptions have volume staying high or irregular, and often growing along with the swings. That is the reverse of most patterns, in which volume fades as the shape forms. It is a tendency in the literature, not a requirement.

Completion and invalidation. Both are harder to define here than for any other pattern on the shelf. The boundaries move away from each other, so a new extreme is part of the pattern and not an exit from it. The neutral description, “swings are expanding”, stops fitting when a swing fails to exceed the previous extreme and the range begins to contract, or when price leaves one boundary and keeps going.

Going deeper

Common false signals. The pattern is close to being a sequence of them. Every move beyond a prior high or low looks like a breakout or breakdown and then reverses, which is the definition of a fakeout. The opposite error also occurs: after several reversals, a reader assumes the next extreme will reverse too, and that is the one that continues.

Top, bottom or neither. Standard texts discuss the shape mainly as a topping formation after a long advance, and describe it as relatively rare. Later catalogues list broadening shapes at bottoms and in the middle of trends as well. The direction of the eventual exit is not something the shape reveals.

Relation to other patterns. A broadening formation that stops expanding becomes a wide rectangle. It is the structural opposite of the symmetrical triangle.

The measured move. Some textbooks describe projecting the height of the formation from the point where price finally leaves it. With boundaries this loose, the convention is even less precise than elsewhere. It is not a target or an expectation.

Pattern recognition is probabilistic and often ambiguous, and the broadening formation is ambiguous by construction.

A hypothetical broadening formation in numbersHypothetical illustration

Price turns at 119, 111, 123, 106, 128 and 101.

  • Highs: 119, 123, 128. Each is higher: by 4, then by 5.
  • Lows: 111, 106, 101. Each is lower: by 5, then by 5.
  • Swing sizes: 119 minus 111 = 8, 123 minus 111 = 12, 123 minus 106 = 17, 128 minus 106 = 22, 128 minus 101 = 27.
  • Full height so far: 128 minus 101 = 27.

A reader who treated the move above 123 as the start of an advance saw price reach 128 and then fall 27 points. The numbers describe expanding swings. They do not indicate whether the next turn comes at the upper line, at the lower line, or not at all.

What it does NOT mean

  • A broadening formation is not a directional pattern. It has higher highs and lower lows at the same time.
  • A new extreme is not an exit. New extremes are what the pattern is made of.
  • The measured move is not a target. It is a loose textbook convention.

Common mistakes

  1. Reading each new high or low as a trend starting. Inside the formation, extremes have been reversing.
  2. Assuming the next extreme must reverse. The alternation ends at some point, without notice.
  3. Drawing precise lines. The swing points seldom line up, and tidy boundaries give a false sense of structure.
  4. Naming it too early. Two highs and two lows in a volatile market are not yet a pattern.

Risks and limitations

  • The pattern is associated with high and rising volatility, the conditions in which price moves are largest and fastest.
  • Completion and invalidation have no widely agreed definition.

Key takeaway

A broadening formation is a market making higher highs and lower lows at once, with swings that keep growing. It is a description of expanding disagreement, and it offers little basis for expecting any particular exit.
