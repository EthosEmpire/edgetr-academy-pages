Cash & No Position

Holding cash and having no open position, often called being flat, is a valid third state beside long and short. It has real costs, and it keeps the option to wait.

Why it matters

Trading screens show two large buttons, one for buying and one for selling. Nothing on the screen represents the third choice, which is to do nothing. That absence shapes how beginners think: it can feel as if every market always requires a position.

It does not. Having no open position is called being flat, and holding cash is one way of being flat. It is a state in the same list as long and short, with consequences of its own.

What it looks like

A long gains if price rises. A short gains if price falls. A flat account does neither. Whatever the market does, the account’s balance in cash terms stays where it was.

In plain words

A driver waiting at a junction for a gap in the traffic has not stopped driving. Waiting is part of the journey, and pulling out at the wrong moment is worse than arriving a minute later. The driver may also decide the road is too busy and take another route.

How to read it: what cash means

In traditional markets, cash means currency in an account or very short-term, low-risk instruments. In crypto, people who say they are “in cash” usually mean one of two things:

  • money in a bank or on an exchange in a national currency;
  • stablecoins, tokens designed to track a currency.

These are not identical. A stablecoin depends on its issuer and its design, and funds on an exchange depend on that exchange. The issues are covered in Stablecoin Risks and Counterparty Risk. Being flat removes exposure to an asset’s price. It does not remove every risk.

The costs of being flat

Opportunity cost. If prices rise while someone is flat, the gain they did not make is the opportunity cost of waiting. It is real in the sense that a different choice would have produced a different result. It is not a loss in the account: no money left it.

Inflation. Cash loses purchasing power when prices in the economy rise. See Interest Rates & Inflation.

Interest paid on cash can offset part or all of this, depending on the rates available at the time.

What being flat provides

  • No market losses. A fall in the asset’s price does not touch a flat account.
  • No carrying costs. There are no trading fees, funding payments or interest charges on a position that does not exist.
  • The option to wait. A flat account can take a position later, at a different price, or never. This flexibility has value even though it does not appear on any statement.

None of this makes being flat the better state. It makes it a state with a different set of trade-offs.

Going deeper

It is not all or nothing. Exposure is a matter of degree. Someone can hold a small position and keep the rest in cash, which is partly long and partly flat.

The pressure to act is psychological. Rising prices produce FOMO. Quiet markets produce boredom, and boredom is a common source of overtrading. Many written trading plans treat “no position” as the default state between planned trades, as discussed in Patience & Discipline.

One flat account, two hypothetical pathsHypothetical illustration

A made-up asset trades at 100. A person considers buying 1 unit and decides to stay flat.

Path A. Over two months the price rises to 120. The opportunity cost of staying flat is 20. The account is unchanged.

Path B. Over two months the price falls to 80. Staying flat avoided an unrealized loss of 20. The account is unchanged.

In both paths the account holds the same cash at the end. Only the comparison differs, and the person could not know on day one which comparison would apply. Judging the decision by Path A alone, or by Path B alone, mistakes the outcome for the quality of the choice.

What it does NOT mean

  • Being flat is not a bet that prices are about to drop. It expresses no direction at all.
  • Being flat is not free. Inflation and opportunity cost are real, even though neither appears as a loss in the account.
  • Cash does not mean no risk. Money on an exchange, and stablecoins, carry issuer and platform risk.
  • A missed gain is not a loss. Treating it as one is a common route into rushed positions.

Common mistakes

  1. Believing every move needs a response. Most price movement requires nothing from an observer.
  2. Opening a position out of boredom. The wish for activity is not information about the market.
  3. Counting missed gains as losses. This turns hindsight into pressure.
  4. Assuming all “cash” is equal. Bank deposits, exchange balances and stablecoins have different risks.

Risks and limitations

  • Purchasing power falls when inflation is higher than the interest earned.
  • Prices may rise for a long time while an account is flat, and the gap cannot be recovered afterwards.
  • “Waiting for a better moment” has no natural end, and can become a permanent state without a deliberate choice.

Key takeaway

Flat is the third position: no gain or loss from price, at the cost of inflation and possible missed gains. It keeps the option to wait, and nothing in a market obliges anyone to be long or short.
