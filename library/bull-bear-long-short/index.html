Bull, Bear, Long & Short

Four words beginners mix up: market conditions versus position direction.

Start here: Bull, Bear, Long & Short Explained

9 pages

Bull, Bear, Long & Short Explained Bull and bear describe a market or an opinion about it, while long and short describe a position someone holds. Keeping the two pairs apart removes a very common beginner confusion. Beginner
Bull Market & Bullish A bull market is a sustained period of generally rising prices, and bullish describes an upward condition or interpretation. Both are descriptions, not instructions to hold a long. Beginner▲ Bullish context
Bear Market & Bearish A bear market is a sustained period of generally falling prices, and bearish describes a downward condition or interpretation. Both are descriptions, not instructions to open a short. Beginner▼ Bearish context
Long Position A long position is one that gains value if the price rises and loses value if it falls. It can be as simple as owning an asset, or it can be a leveraged contract with very different risks. Beginner▲ Bullish context
Short Position A short position is one that gains value if the price falls and loses value if it rises. It relies on borrowing or on a derivative contract, and its possible loss is not capped. Beginner▼ Bearish context
Cash & No Position Holding cash and having no open position, often called being flat, is a valid third state beside long and short. It has real costs, and it keeps the option to wait. Beginner
Market Cycles A market cycle is a descriptive model with four phases: accumulation, advance, distribution and decline. The phases are clear in hindsight and cannot be timed reliably. Intermediate
Short Squeeze A short squeeze is a fast price rise in which short sellers are forced to buy back their positions, and their buying pushes the price higher still. This page explains the mechanism only. Intermediate
Hedging Basics Hedging means reducing exposure to a price move by holding an offsetting position. It lowers both losses and gains, and it has costs and risks of its own, so it is not free protection. Advanced
