Chart Types: Line, Bar, Candle & More

Chart types are different ways of drawing the same price data. Line, bar, candlestick, Heikin Ashi, area and Renko charts each keep some information and leave some out.

  • 5 min read

Why it matters

Before any analysis happens, a choice has already been made: how the price data is drawn. Most platforms open on candlesticks, and offer several other chart types from a single menu.

Each type is a different drawing of the same trades. Some show every detail of a period, some show one number, and some transform the data before drawing it. Knowing what each one leaves out prevents reading something into a chart that is only an effect of the drawing.

About the platform named here

TradingView is used as the familiar example. edgeTR Academy is not affiliated with TradingView, and the same ideas apply to other charting tools.

What it looks like

Type What is drawn What is left out
Line One price per period, usually the close, joined by a line Open, high and low
Bar (OHLC) A vertical line from low to high, a tick on the left for the open and on the right for the close Nothing
Candlestick The same four prices, with a filled body between open and close Nothing
Heikin Ashi Candles built from averaged values The actual open, high, low and close
Renko Bricks of a fixed price size Time, and any move smaller than one brick

In plain words

A journey can be recorded as a list of arrival times, as a full logbook, or as a smoothed route on a map. All three describe the same trip. The list is quick to read and hides the detours. The smoothed route is pleasant to look at and no longer shows the exact road taken.

How to read it

Line and area

A line chart joins closing prices. It is the cleanest view of general direction. It hides everything that happened inside each period, including the wicks that show how far price travelled before closing. An area chart is a line chart with shading and carries the same information.

Bars and candlesticks

An OHLC bar and a candlestick show the same four numbers: open, high, low and close. The candlestick adds a coloured body, which makes the direction of each period and the relation between open and close visible at a glance. The parts are explained in Candle Anatomy.

Heikin Ashi

Heikin Ashi is a Japanese name usually translated as “average bar”. The candles look like ordinary candlesticks and are built from formulas:

  • HA close = (open + high + low + close) / 4
  • HA open = (previous HA open + previous HA close) / 2
  • HA high = the highest of the real high, HA open and HA close
  • HA low = the lowest of the real low, HA open and HA close

Because each candle is tied to the one before, trends appear as long runs of one colour with few interruptions. That smoothing has a price. The open and close shown are averages, so they are not prices at which anything traded. Price gaps disappear. The last value on the chart can differ from the current market price.

Renko

A Renko chart ignores time. A new brick is drawn only when price has moved by a set amount, the brick size, from the previous brick. During a quiet week the chart may add nothing. During a violent hour it may add many bricks. The horizontal axis is therefore uneven in time.

Renko removes small fluctuations by construction. It also removes the information of how long a move took, and the picture changes with the brick size chosen. Point and figure charts, an older method, follow the same time-independent idea.

Going deeper

The data is the same. Switching chart type changes what is drawn. It does not change what happened. If two types seem to tell different stories, the difference comes from what each one omits or averages.

Indicators may follow the chart type. On some platforms, an indicator added to a Heikin Ashi or Renko chart is calculated from the transformed values, so its output differs from the same indicator on a standard chart.

Candlestick patterns need real candles. Named patterns such as the hammer are defined on actual open, high, low and close values.

One hypothetical period, drawn two waysHypothetical illustration

The previous Heikin Ashi candle had an open of 100 and a close of 102. The current real candle, as a standard chart shows it, is: open 103, high 106, low 101, close 105.

  • HA close = (103 + 106 + 101 + 105) / 4 = 415 / 4 = 103.75
  • HA open = (100 + 102) / 2 = 101
  • HA high = highest of 106, 101 and 103.75 = 106
  • HA low = lowest of 101, 101 and 103.75 = 101

The real candle runs from an open of 103 to a close of 105. The Heikin Ashi candle runs from 101 to 103.75. Both are bullish. The market’s last traded price is 105, and the Heikin Ashi chart shows 103.75 as its close.

What it does NOT mean

  • A smoother chart is not a more accurate chart. Smoothing removes detail. It does not add information.
  • A run of same-coloured Heikin Ashi candles is not a forecast. It describes averaged past prices.
  • A line chart is not the full record. Highs and lows inside each period are absent.
  • No chart type is the correct one. Each is a view chosen for a purpose.

Common mistakes

  1. Reading exact prices from Heikin Ashi candles. Their open and close are calculated, not traded.
  2. Forgetting that a line chart hides wicks. A level that looks untouched on a line chart may have been crossed within a period.
  3. Treating Renko brick size as neutral. A different size produces a different chart.

Risks and limitations

  • Averaged and time-independent charts can show a price that differs from the live market.
  • Exact calculation details for transformed charts can differ between platforms.
  • A cleaner picture can encourage more confidence than the data supports.

Key takeaway

Line, area, bar, candlestick, Heikin Ashi and Renko charts are different drawings of the same trades. Candlesticks and bars show every period’s four prices, while the others simplify, average or drop time, so it helps to know what a chart type hides before reading it.
