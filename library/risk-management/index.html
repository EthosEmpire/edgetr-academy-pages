Risk Management

Position sizing, stop placement, risk/reward, drawdown and survival.

Start here: What Is Risk Management?

13 pages

What Is Risk Management? Risk management is the set of decisions, made before the outcome is known, that limit how much a single trade or a whole account can lose. Beginner
Risk Per Trade Risk per trade is the amount an account would lose if one trade is closed at its planned stop, usually expressed as a percentage of the account. Beginner
Position Sizing Position sizing is the calculation of how large a trade can be so that the loss at the planned stop equals the amount chosen as risk per trade. Beginner
Risk/Reward Ratio The risk/reward ratio compares the planned loss on a trade, from entry to stop, with the planned gain, from entry to target. Beginner
Stop Placement Stop placement is the choice of the price at which a trade idea is treated as wrong, and it sets the stop distance that position sizing depends on. Intermediate
Drawdown A drawdown is the decline of an account or an asset from a peak to a later low, measured as a percentage of the peak. Beginner
Win Rate & Expectancy Win rate is the share of trades that end in a gain. Expectancy combines it with the average size of wins and losses to give the average result per trade. Intermediate
Leverage Risk Leverage risk is the way borrowed exposure multiplies losses on a trader's own capital and adds the possibility of forced closure, called liquidation. Intermediate
Diversification & Correlation Diversification spreads capital across holdings that do not all move together. Correlation, from -1 to +1, measures how closely two of them have moved together. Intermediate
Concentration Risk Concentration risk is the danger that comes from having a large share of capital depend on a single asset, theme, venue or outcome. Intermediate
Risk of Ruin Risk of ruin is the probability that an account falls to a level where trading cannot continue, given a win rate, a payoff ratio and a risk per trade. Advanced
Trading Plan A trading plan is a written set of rules, made in advance, that describes what a person trades, when, how much is at risk and how each trade ends. Beginner
Trading Journal A trading journal is a record of each trade's reasoning, risk, result and circumstances, kept so that decisions can be reviewed against evidence and not memory. Beginner
