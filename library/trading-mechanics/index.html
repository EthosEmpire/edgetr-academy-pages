Trading Mechanics

Orders, spot and perpetuals, leverage, margin, funding, liquidation and P&L.

Start here: How a Trade Works

19 pages

How a Trade Works A trade starts as an order, becomes a fill when it is matched with another trader's order, lives as an open position, and ends when that position is closed. Beginner
Order Book An order book is the live list of all waiting buy and sell orders for one market, sorted by price, from which the exchange matches trades. Beginner
Bid, Ask & Spread The bid is the highest price a buyer is offering, the ask is the lowest price a seller will accept, and the spread is the gap between the two. Beginner
Order Types An order type is the set of conditions attached to an order: whether it trades immediately, only at a chosen price, or only after a trigger price is reached. Beginner
Market Orders A market order is an instruction to buy or sell immediately at the best prices currently available, accepting whatever those prices turn out to be. Beginner
Limit Orders A limit order is an instruction to buy or sell only at a stated price or better, which fixes the worst acceptable price but leaves the fill uncertain. Beginner
Stop Orders A stop order stays dormant until the market reaches a trigger price, then becomes a market order (stop-market) or a limit order (stop-limit). Beginner
Slippage Slippage is the difference between the price a trader expected when sending an order and the price at which the order was actually filled. Beginner
Trading Fees: Maker & Taker Exchanges charge a fee on each fill: the maker rate applies to orders that add resting liquidity to the book and the taker rate to orders that remove it. Beginner
Entry & Exit The entry is the fill that opens a position and the exit is the fill that closes it; together they fix the result of a trade before costs. Beginner
Stop Loss A stop loss is a stop order placed in advance to close a position if price moves against it to a chosen level, limiting the loss approximately but not exactly. Beginner
Take Profit A take profit is an order placed in advance to close a position at a favourable price, turning an unrealized gain into a realized one if the market reaches that level. Beginner
P&L: Realized & Unrealized Unrealized P&L is the gain or loss on a position that is still open and changes with price; realized P&L is the gain or loss locked in when a position is closed. Beginner
Spot Trading Spot trading is buying or selling the asset itself, paid for in full and delivered straight away, with no borrowing, no leverage and no liquidation. Beginner
Margin Trading Margin trading means trading with funds borrowed from an exchange or broker against collateral, which enlarges a position and multiplies both its gains and its losses. Intermediate
Leverage Leverage is the ratio of a position's size to the margin backing it; it multiplies the effect of every price move on that margin, for losses exactly as much as for gains. Intermediate
Margin & Collateral Margin is the collateral posted to open and keep a leveraged position; it absorbs the position's losses, and if it falls below the maintenance requirement the position is liquidated. Intermediate
Isolated & Cross Margin Isolated margin normally confines a position's loss to the margin assigned to it, while cross margin lets positions draw on the whole account balance as shared collateral. Intermediate
Liquidation Liquidation is the forced closing of a leveraged position by the exchange when the margin behind it falls below the maintenance requirement. Intermediate
