Indicators

What each line measures, how it is calculated, common settings and what it cannot tell you.

Start here: How Indicators Work

26 pages

How Indicators Work A technical indicator is a calculation applied to past price and volume data, drawn on the chart to make one aspect of market behaviour easier to see. Beginner
Moving Averages A moving average is a line that shows the average price over a set number of recent candles, smoothing out short-term noise so the general direction is easier to see. Beginner
SMA (Simple Moving Average) The simple moving average is the plain arithmetic mean of the last N closing prices, with every close in the window given exactly the same weight. Beginner
EMA (Exponential Moving Average) The exponential moving average is a moving average that gives the most weight to the newest closing prices, so it reacts to changes sooner than a simple average. Beginner
Moving Average Crossovers A moving average crossover is the moment a faster average, or price itself, moves from one side of a slower average to the other, recording a shift that has already happened. Intermediate
RSI (Relative Strength Index) RSI is a momentum indicator that compares recent gains with recent losses and shows the result on a scale from 0 to 100. Beginner
MACD MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence) plots the gap between a fast and a slow exponential moving average, showing whether that gap is growing or shrinking. Beginner
Stochastic Oscillator The Stochastic Oscillator shows where the latest close sits within the high-low range of recent candles, on a scale from 0 (at the low) to 100 (at the high). Intermediate
Divergence Divergence is a disagreement between price and an indicator: price makes a new high or low, but the indicator does not, which is commonly read as momentum fading. Intermediate
Bollinger Bands Bollinger Bands are a 20-period moving average with an upper and a lower band set two standard deviations away, so the bands widen and narrow as volatility changes. Beginner
ATR (Average True Range) ATR is a volatility indicator that shows the average size of recent candles, including gaps, in price units. It measures how much price moves, not which way. Beginner
Volume Volume is the amount of an asset traded during each candle, shown as bars beneath the price chart. It measures how much activity stood behind a price move. Beginner
VWAP VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price) is the average price traded during a session, with each price weighted by the volume traded at it. Intermediate
OBV (On-Balance Volume) On-Balance Volume is a running total that adds a period's volume when price closes higher and subtracts it when price closes lower. Intermediate
Volume Profile Volume profile is a sideways histogram that shows how much volume traded at each price level over a chosen period, instead of how much traded in each time period. Advanced
ADX (Average Directional Index) ADX measures how strong a trend has been on a scale from 0 to 100, without saying which way it points; its companion lines +DI and -DI describe direction. Intermediate
Rate of Change (ROC) Rate of Change is a momentum indicator that shows, as a percentage, how far the current close is above or below the close a fixed number of periods ago. Intermediate
Keltner Channels Keltner Channels are a volatility envelope: an exponential moving average with an upper and a lower line placed a multiple of the Average True Range away from it. Intermediate
Donchian Channels Donchian Channels plot the highest high and the lowest low of the last N periods, so the band shows the full price range the market has covered recently. Intermediate
Supertrend Supertrend is a trend-following line built from the Average True Range that trails below price in rising phases and flips above price when a candle closes through it. Intermediate
Parabolic SAR Parabolic SAR is a trend-following indicator that plots trailing dots below or above price; the dots speed up as a move extends and switch sides when price reaches them. Intermediate
Money Flow Index (MFI) The Money Flow Index is a 0-100 oscillator that applies the RSI idea to price multiplied by volume, so periods with heavy volume count for more than quiet ones. Advanced
CCI (Commodity Channel Index) CCI measures how far the typical price is from its moving average, scaled by how far it usually strays; it has no fixed bounds and uses +100 and -100 as reference levels. Advanced
Williams %R Williams %R shows where the latest close sits within the high-low range of the last N periods, on a scale from 0 at the top of the range to -100 at the bottom. Advanced
Combining Indicators Combining indicators means choosing tools that measure different things, because indicators from the same family are built from the same data and repeat the same information. Intermediate
Lagging vs Leading Indicators Lagging indicators follow price and confirm moves after they happen, while so-called leading indicators react sooner; both are calculated from past data, and neither predicts. Intermediate
