Trading Styles

Scalping, day, swing and position trading, and the approaches used within them.

Start here: Overview of Trading Styles

13 pages

Overview of Trading Styles A trading style describes how long positions are usually held and what kind of price behaviour an approach is built around. No single style suits everyone. Beginner
Trading and Investing Trading and investing both mean buying and selling assets. They differ in time horizon, in what a decision is based on, and in the costs and attention they demand. Beginner
Scalping Scalping is a style built on very short holding periods, from seconds to a few minutes, and very small price moves. It is highly sensitive to fees, spread and execution. Intermediate
Day Trading Day trading is a style in which positions are opened and closed within the same day, so nothing is held overnight. It demands hours of attention and many trades. Beginner
Swing Trading Swing trading is a style in which positions are held for days to weeks, with the aim of taking part in one swing of price inside a larger move. Beginner
Position Trading Position trading is a style in which positions are held for weeks to months or longer, with attention on the larger trend and little on day-to-day movement. Beginner
Trend Following Trend following is an approach that acts in the direction of an established trend and makes no attempt to predict tops or bottoms. It endures many small losses in sideways markets. Beginner
Momentum Trading Momentum trading is an approach built on the idea that a strong, fast price move may continue for a while. Its characteristic risk is a sharp reversal once the move is exhausted. Intermediate
Breakout Trading Breakout trading is an approach built around price leaving a range, level or pattern, on the idea that a new move may follow. Its characteristic cost is the failed breakout. Intermediate
Pullback Trading Pullback trading is an approach that waits for a temporary move against an established trend, on the idea that the trend may resume. Its risk is a pullback that becomes a reversal. Intermediate
Range Trading Range trading is an approach built on price moving back and forth between a support area and a resistance area. It relies on the boundaries holding, and every range eventually ends. Intermediate
Mean Reversion Mean reversion is an approach built on the idea that a price stretched far from its average may move back toward it. Its characteristic risk is a trend that does not revert. Intermediate
Choosing a Timeframe A chart timeframe sets how much time each candle covers. The timeframe a trader uses usually follows from the holding period, the time available and the weight of costs. Beginner
