Crypto Markets and Stock Markets

Stock markets have set trading hours, closing auctions and separate brokers, exchanges and clearing houses. Crypto spot markets trade around the clock on platforms that often combine those roles.

Why it matters

Many people meet trading through crypto first and assume all markets work the same way. Others arrive from stocks and expect crypto to follow the same rules. Charts from the two worlds look alike, and the same indicators run on both.

Underneath, the two are organised quite differently. Those differences affect what a chart shows, when prices can move and whom a participant relies on. This page describes them without ranking them.

The differences at a glance

In plain words

A stock exchange is like a covered market hall with opening hours, a manager and inspectors. When it shuts, trading stops until morning. A crypto market is like an open square that never closes, with many separate stalls. You can trade at three in the morning on a Sunday. There is also nobody to lock the doors when things get rough.

Trading hours

A stock exchange opens and closes at fixed times on business days. The main session of the large US exchanges, for example, runs from 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time. Many exchanges start and end the day with an auction, in which orders are collected and matched at a single price. The closing auction produces the official closing price, used to value funds and calculate indexes.

Crypto spot markets do not close. Trading continues through nights, weekends and holidays, as described in A Market That Never Closes. A “daily” crypto candle is simply a 24-hour slice starting at a time the chart provider chose, commonly midnight UTC.

What this does to a chart

News does not wait for opening hours. When a stock market reopens, the price can start well away from the previous close, leaving a gap on the chart. No trades happened at the prices in between. In a market that never closes, the price moves through those levels with trades along the way, although it can still move very fast.

What is being traded

A share is a legal claim on a company, with rights defined by law. A crypto asset is a unit on a blockchain whose properties are defined by its protocol or issuer. Some crypto assets are designed as money, some pay for network use, some grant votes in a project. Few resemble shares. See Coins & Tokens.

Going deeper

Who stands in the middle

In stock markets, the broker, the exchange, the clearing house and the custodian are usually separate, regulated organisations, as explained in Exchanges & Brokers. A centralized crypto exchange often performs all four roles in one company. Crypto holders also have an option that is uncommon for shareholders: withdrawing the asset and holding it themselves, covered in Custodial & Non-Custodial Wallets.

Pauses

Stock exchanges can halt trading in a single share around major news, and many have circuit breakers that pause the whole market after a very large fall. Crypto spot markets generally have no market-wide equivalent. An individual exchange may suspend trading or go offline, while trading carries on elsewhere.

One price or many

A share usually has a main listing, and in many countries rules link the prices on different venues closely. A crypto asset trades on many independent exchanges worldwide, each with its own order book. Arbitrage keeps their prices near each other, not identical.

Rules and disclosure

Listed companies must publish audited accounts and report significant events. Requirements for crypto projects vary widely by jurisdiction and are still developing. On the other hand, every transfer on a public blockchain is visible to anyone, which has no direct equivalent in stock markets.

Size of moves

Many crypto assets have historically shown larger percentage swings than broad stock indexes. That is an observation about the past, and volatility is not a fixed property of either market.

The same hypothetical weekend news in two marketsHypothetical illustration

A share and a coin both trade at 100 on Friday evening. On Saturday, news arrives that participants take badly.

The share. The exchange is closed. Nothing trades on Saturday or Sunday. On Monday the opening auction sets a price of 94. The chart shows a gap from 100 to 94. A holder who wanted to sell at 98 had no opportunity, because no trade took place there.

The coin. Trading continues. Over Saturday the price moves from 100 down to 94, with trades at many levels on the way. A holder could have sold at 98 if they were watching and buyers were there at that moment. Weekend trading is often thinner, so moves can be sharp.

Both end at 94. The paths differ, and so does what each chart records.

What it does NOT mean

  • Continuous trading does not mean an exit at any price you choose. Trading is always possible only at prices where someone else is willing to trade.
  • Regulation does not make shares free of risk. Share prices fall and companies fail under any rulebook.
  • A token is not a share of a crypto project. Most tokens carry no legal claim on a company’s profits or assets.
  • Similar-looking charts do not mean similar markets. Session gaps, closing prices and volume figures are built differently.
  • Neither structure is better by definition. Each involves different conveniences, protections and risks.

Common misunderstandings

  1. Expecting a stock to trade at night like a coin. Outside the main session, trading is limited or absent.
  2. Treating a crypto “daily close” as an official price. It is a convention of the chart, and it differs between providers.
  3. Assuming stock market protections apply to crypto platforms. Whether they do depends on the jurisdiction and the platform.

Limits

  • This page compares typical spot markets. Derivatives such as perpetual futures add further differences.
  • Market structures are changing. Some stock venues are extending their hours, and crypto regulation is developing.
  • Details vary greatly by country, exchange and asset.

Key takeaway

Stock markets run in sessions with official closes and several separate intermediaries. Crypto spot markets run continuously on many independent venues that often combine those roles. The charts look similar and the plumbing does not.
