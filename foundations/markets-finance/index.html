Markets & Finance

Assets, order books, liquidity, market cap and who takes part in markets.

Start here: What Is a Market?

20 pages

What Is a Market? A market is any place or system where buyers and sellers meet to exchange something at a price they both accept. Beginner
Buyers, Sellers & Price A price is the level of the most recent trade between a buyer and a seller. It moves when one side becomes more willing to accept the other side's terms. Beginner
Supply & Demand Supply is how much of something sellers offer at each price, demand is how much buyers want at each price, and the market price settles where the two balance. Beginner
Assets & Asset Classes An asset is something of economic value that can be owned and traded. Asset classes are groups of assets that share the same basic features and behave in broadly similar ways. Beginner
Stocks & Shares A share is one unit of ownership in a company. Stocks trade on exchanges, and their prices reflect what buyers and sellers currently agree the company's shares are worth. Beginner
Bonds A bond is a loan to a government or company that pays interest and returns the amount borrowed on a set date. Its market price moves in the opposite direction to interest rates. Beginner
ETFs & Indexes An index is a calculated number that tracks a defined group of assets. An ETF is a fund whose shares trade on an exchange, and most ETFs are built to follow an index. Beginner
Commodities Commodities are standardised raw materials such as oil, gold, copper and wheat. They trade in physical markets and, more visibly, through futures contracts. Beginner
Forex & Currency Pairs Forex is the market where currencies are exchanged for one another. A quote such as EUR/USD gives the price of one unit of the first currency in terms of the second. Beginner
Exchanges & Brokers An exchange is a venue where buy and sell orders are matched under published rules. A broker is an intermediary that gives customers access to that venue and handles their orders. Beginner
Market Capitalization Market capitalization is the current price of one unit multiplied by the number of units outstanding. It measures size at the latest price, not the amount of money invested. Beginner
Liquidity Liquidity is how easily an asset can be bought or sold, in the size you want, without the trade itself moving the price. Beginner
Trading Volume Explained Trading volume is the number of units of an asset that changed hands in a period. Every unit counted was bought by one party and sold by another. Beginner
Volatility Volatility is a measure of how large an asset's price moves are over a period. It describes the size of the swings, not their direction. Beginner
Market Participants Market participants are the people and organisations that trade: investors, hedgers, speculators, arbitrageurs and market makers, each with different goals and time horizons. Beginner
Market Makers & Liquidity Providers A market maker continuously quotes a price to buy and a price to sell an asset, earning the spread between them in exchange for carrying the risk of holding inventory. Intermediate
Arbitrage Arbitrage is buying an asset where it is cheaper and selling the same asset where it is dearer. The trading itself tends to remove the price difference. Intermediate
Interest Rates & Inflation Inflation is a general rise in prices that reduces what money can buy. Interest rates are the price of borrowing money, and central banks adjust their policy rates largely in response to inflation. Intermediate
Crypto Markets and Stock Markets Stock markets have set trading hours, closing auctions and separate brokers, exchanges and clearing houses. Crypto spot markets trade around the clock on platforms that often combine those roles. Beginner
Technical & Fundamental Analysis Technical analysis studies price and volume history. Fundamental analysis studies the underlying business or asset. They ask different questions, and neither produces certainty. Beginner
