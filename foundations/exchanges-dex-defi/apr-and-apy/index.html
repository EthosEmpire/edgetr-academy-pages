APR & APY

APR is a yearly rate that leaves compounding out, and APY is a yearly rate that includes it, so the same underlying rate gives a higher APY than APR.

  • Beginner
  • 5 min read
  • Updated 21 November 2026
On this page
  1. Why it matters
  2. How it works
  3. How it is calculated
  4. Going deeper
  5. What it does NOT mean

Why it matters

Almost every staking, lending and liquidity product is advertised with a yearly percentage. Some quote APR and some quote APY, and the two are not interchangeable. A product can look better than another simply because it uses the label that produces the bigger number.

Both figures also share a deeper limit: they describe a rate, not a result.

How it works

APR, the annual percentage rate, is a yearly rate with no compounding. At 12% APR, 1,000 earns 120 over a year, and that is all.

APY, the annual percentage yield, includes compounding: each payment is added to the balance, so later payments are calculated on a larger amount. At 12% APR paid monthly, the first month pays 1% of 1,000, the second pays 1% of 1,010, and so on.

In plain words

APR is like being paid the same wage every month. APY is like getting a small raise every month, where each raise is worked out from the wage you had just reached. The more often the raise is applied, the more it adds up to, though with diminishing effect.

How it is calculated

APY = (1 + r / n)^n - 1r is the APR written as a decimal. n is the number of compounding periods in a year.

For a 12% APR compounded monthly: (1 + 0.12 / 12)^12 - 1 = 1.01^12 - 1 = 0.1268, or about 12.68%.

APR APY with daily compounding
5% about 5.13%
12% about 12.75%
50% about 64.8%
100% about 171.5%

The conversion runs both ways. A product quoting 100% APY with daily compounding is paying an APR of about 69.4%. Comparing a figure in APR with another in APY is comparing different units.

Going deeper

How a quoted rate is produced

Crypto rates are rarely contractual. Most are annualized from a short window: the rewards of the last day or week, scaled up to a year. If a pool paid 0.1% yesterday, an interface may show 36.5% APR (0.1% x 365), or about 44% APY if it assumes daily compounding. Both figures assume that yesterday repeats 365 times.

A quoted rate is not a promise

Quoted APR and APY figures are variable and are not guaranteed. They can change within hours as deposits, trading volume or reward programs change. They are often paid in a token whose price can fall, so a positive rate can go together with a loss in ordinary money. And every rate comes with the risks of the mechanism producing it: staking, lending or liquidity provision.

Compounding is not always automatic

An APY figure assumes rewards are reinvested at the stated frequency. In many protocols that takes a transaction each time, with a network fee. For a small balance, the fees of compounding daily can exceed what compounding adds. Other protocols compound automatically inside the contract.

The unit problem

A rate is expressed in the asset being paid. A 12.68% APY in a token means 12.68% more tokens. If the token loses 30% of its price, the position is worth less than at the start, as the figure shows. Rewards funded by issuing new tokens add to supply, which is one source of that price pressure. See token unlocks and emissions.

What the rate leaves out

The headline figure does not subtract impermanent loss, network fees, validator commissions or slashing. It also takes no account of inflation in the currency used to measure it.

Borrowing

The same arithmetic applies to debt. A borrowing rate quoted as APR understates the cost if interest is added to the debt and itself accrues interest. In consumer lending, many jurisdictions regulate how APR must be calculated. In crypto there is no common standard, so the method belongs to whoever displays the number.

The same hypothetical rate, quoted two waysHypothetical illustration

Made-up numbers. 1,000 is deposited at a rate of 12% a year that, for simplicity, never changes.

As APR, with no compounding.

  • Interest for the year: 1,000 x 0.12 = 120.
  • Balance: 1,120.

Compounded monthly.

  • Month 1: 1% of 1,000 = 10.00. Balance 1,010.00.
  • Month 2: 1% of 1,010 = 10.10. Balance 1,020.10.
  • After 12 months: 1,000 x 1.01^12 = 1,126.83.

Compounding added 6.83 over the year. An interface could truthfully describe this product as “12% APR” or as “12.68% APY”.

Now suppose the balance is held in a token that started at a price of 1.00 and ended the year at 0.70. The 1,126.83 tokens are worth 1,126.83 x 0.70 = 788.78, which is about 21% less than the 1,000 at the start.

What it does NOT mean

  • A high APY does not mean a high return. It is a rate in tokens, before price changes, costs and losses.
  • APY is not a better deal than APR. It is the same rate expressed with compounding included.
  • A quoted rate is not fixed. In crypto it is usually a snapshot of recent activity.
  • A rate is not earned by doing nothing. It is payment for carrying the risks of the product behind it.
  • A very large percentage is not generosity. It usually reflects a small pool, a new token being issued in volume, or a short window scaled up to a year.

Common misunderstandings

  1. Comparing an APR with an APY. Both figures need converting to the same basis first. See APR vs APY.
  2. Reading a snapshot as a forecast. The annualized figure assumes conditions that rarely last a week, let alone a year.
  3. Forgetting what the rate is paid in. The value of the reward depends on the token’s price when it is received.
  4. Ignoring compounding costs. Each manual reinvestment costs a network fee.
  5. Leaving out the losses. A rate does not include impermanent loss, slashing, liquidation or contract failure.

Risks and limits

  • Displayed rates differ between interfaces, because each uses its own window and method.
  • Rates on new or small pools are the least stable and the most flattering.
  • Reward tokens can lose value faster than they are paid out.
  • Neither figure measures the chance of losing the deposit itself.

Key takeaway

APR is a yearly rate without compounding and APY is the same rate with compounding: 12% APR compounded monthly is about 12.68% APY. Both are variable estimates in tokens, and neither says what a position will be worth.
