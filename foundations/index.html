Markets & Finance

Assets, order books, liquidity, market cap and who takes part in markets.

All 20 pages
What Is a Market? A market is any place or system where buyers and sellers meet to exchange something at a price they both accept. Beginner Buyers, Sellers & Price A price is the level of the most recent trade between a buyer and a seller. It moves when one side becomes more willing to accept the other side's terms. Beginner Supply & Demand Supply is how much of something sellers offer at each price, demand is how much buyers want at each price, and the market price settles where the two balance. Beginner Assets & Asset Classes An asset is something of economic value that can be owned and traded. Asset classes are groups of assets that share the same basic features and behave in broadly similar ways. Beginner

Crypto Basics

Coins, tokens, stablecoins, supply and the vocabulary of crypto.

All 14 pages
What Is Cryptocurrency? A cryptocurrency is a digital asset whose ownership records are kept on a blockchain, a shared ledger run by a network of computers instead of a single bank or company. Beginner Coins & Tokens A coin is the native asset of its own blockchain, while a token is issued by a smart contract that runs on an existing blockchain and relies on that chain's coin for fees. Beginner Altcoins Altcoin is a loose label for any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin, covering thousands of assets with very different purposes, designs, liquidity and risks. Beginner Stablecoins A stablecoin is a token designed to track a reference value, usually one U.S. dollar, using reserves, crypto collateral or an algorithm, each with its own way of failing. Beginner

Bitcoin Knowledge Center

A neutral technical history: the white paper, mining, the 21 million limit, halvings and nodes.

All 15 pages
What Is Bitcoin? Bitcoin is a digital currency and payment network that runs without a central operator, using a public ledger that thousands of independent computers verify. Beginner Satoshi Nakamoto Satoshi Nakamoto is the name used by the unidentified person or group who designed Bitcoin, wrote its white paper and released its first software. Beginner The Bitcoin White Paper The Bitcoin white paper is the nine-page document from 2008 in which Satoshi Nakamoto described how digital cash could work without a trusted third party. Beginner Genesis Block & Early History The genesis block is the first block of the Bitcoin blockchain, dated 3 January 2009. This page follows the network from that block through its first few years. Beginner

Blockchain

Blocks, transactions, consensus, smart contracts, Layer 1 and Layer 2.

All 12 pages
What Is a Blockchain? A blockchain is a shared ledger in which records are grouped into blocks, each block is linked to the one before it by a cryptographic hash, and many independent computers hold a copy. Beginner Blocks & Hashes A block is a batch of transactions plus the hash of the previous block. A cryptographic hash is a fixed-length fingerprint of data, in which any change to the input changes the output. Beginner Transactions A blockchain transaction is a signed instruction, such as a transfer of value, that is broadcast to the network, checked by nodes and recorded in the ledger once it is in a block, and increasingly hard to reverse. Beginner Nodes, Validators & Miners Nodes store and verify a blockchain's ledger. Miners, under proof of work, and validators, under proof of stake, are the participants that propose new blocks for the nodes to check. Beginner

Wallets & Security

Keys, seed phrases, custody, approvals and how common scams work.

All 17 pages
What Is a Crypto Wallet? A crypto wallet is software or a device that stores and uses the private keys controlling funds on a blockchain. The coins themselves are records on the blockchain, not files in the wallet. Beginner Private Keys A private key is a secret number that authorises spending from a blockchain address. Whoever has the key controls the funds, and nobody can reset or reissue it. Beginner Seed Phrases A seed phrase is a list of words, commonly 12 or 24, from which a wallet can regenerate all of its private keys. It is the master secret and the only backup of the wallet. Beginner Custodial & Non-Custodial Wallets In a custodial wallet a company holds the private keys for its customers. In a non-custodial wallet the user holds them. The difference decides who can move, freeze or lose the funds. Beginner

Exchanges, DEX & DeFi

CEXs, DEXs, AMMs, liquidity pools, lending and the risks specific to DeFi.

All 14 pages
Centralized Exchanges (CEX) A centralized exchange is a company that runs a crypto market: it holds customers' funds, records their balances in its own ledger and matches their buy and sell orders. Beginner Decentralized Exchanges (DEX) A decentralized exchange is a set of smart contracts on a blockchain that lets people trade tokens directly from their own wallets, with no company holding their funds. Beginner What Is DeFi? DeFi, short for decentralized finance, is the collective name for financial services such as trading, lending and borrowing that run as smart contracts on public blockchains. Beginner AMMs & Liquidity Pools An automated market maker (AMM) prices trades with a formula applied to a pool of two tokens, instead of matching buyers and sellers in an order book. Intermediate

Derivatives & Perpetuals

Futures, perpetuals, options basics, funding, open interest and mark price.

All 12 pages
What Are Derivatives? A derivative is a contract whose value comes from the price of something else, called the underlying asset, rather than from owning that asset directly. Beginner Futures Contracts A futures contract is a standardised agreement to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a set future date, traded on an exchange and backed by margin. Intermediate Perpetual Futures A perpetual future is a futures contract with no expiry date that uses funding payments between longs and shorts to keep its price close to the spot index. Intermediate Funding Rate The funding rate is a periodic payment exchanged between long and short holders of a perpetual future that pulls the contract price toward the spot index. Intermediate
