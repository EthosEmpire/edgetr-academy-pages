Blockchain

Blocks, transactions, consensus, smart contracts, Layer 1 and Layer 2.

Start here: What Is a Blockchain?

12 pages

What Is a Blockchain? A blockchain is a shared ledger in which records are grouped into blocks, each block is linked to the one before it by a cryptographic hash, and many independent computers hold a copy. Beginner
Blocks & Hashes A block is a batch of transactions plus the hash of the previous block. A cryptographic hash is a fixed-length fingerprint of data, in which any change to the input changes the output. Beginner
Transactions A blockchain transaction is a signed instruction, such as a transfer of value, that is broadcast to the network, checked by nodes and recorded in the ledger once it is in a block, and increasingly hard to reverse. Beginner
Nodes, Validators & Miners Nodes store and verify a blockchain's ledger. Miners, under proof of work, and validators, under proof of stake, are the participants that propose new blocks for the nodes to check. Beginner
Consensus Consensus is the process by which the independent computers of a blockchain network agree on a single history of transactions without relying on a central authority. Beginner
Proof of Work Proof of work secures a blockchain by making block production cost computation and energy: miners compete to find a block hash below a target, and rewriting history means redoing that work. Intermediate
Proof of Stake Proof of stake secures a blockchain with locked capital: validators deposit the network's coin as a stake, are chosen to propose and vote on blocks, and can lose that stake for misbehaving. Intermediate
Smart Contracts A smart contract is a program stored on a blockchain that runs exactly as written when a transaction calls it. Its code and balances are public, and bugs in it can be exploited. Beginner
Layer 1 & Layer 2 A Layer 1 is a base blockchain that secures and settles transactions itself. A Layer 2 processes transactions off that base chain and settles the results to it, to increase capacity. Intermediate
Bridges A blockchain bridge moves value between two chains, usually by locking an asset on one and issuing a matching token on the other. Bridges have been frequent targets of large exploits. Intermediate
Finality & Throughput Finality is the point after which a transaction is practically irreversible. Throughput is how many transactions a chain processes per second. Both trade off against decentralisation and security. Advanced
Public & Private Blockchains A public blockchain lets anyone read, transact and help validate. A private or permissioned blockchain restricts those roles to approved participants chosen by an operator or consortium. Intermediate
