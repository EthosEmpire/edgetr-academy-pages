Custodial & Non-Custodial Wallets

In a custodial wallet a company holds the private keys for its customers. In a non-custodial wallet the user holds them. The difference decides who can move, freeze or lose the funds.

Why it matters

“Wallet” is used for two very different arrangements. In one, a company keeps the private keys and the customer has an account. In the other, the user keeps the keys and nobody else is involved. They can look almost identical on a phone screen.

The difference decides what happens when something goes wrong: a forgotten password, a failed company, a stolen login or a lost backup. Each arrangement fails in its own way.

How it works

Custodial means someone else has custody of the keys. The usual example is an account on a centralized exchange or with a payment app. The customer signs in with an email address and a password, and the company signs the blockchain transactions.

Non-custodial, also called self-custody, means the keys are generated and stored on the user’s own device, backed up by a seed phrase. In a standard wallet, no company can move the funds, and no company can restore them.

In plain words

A custodial account is like money in a bank. The bank keeps it, records what it owes, and can help if a card is lost. Unlike a bank deposit, a custodial crypto balance is generally not covered by deposit insurance. A non-custodial wallet is like cash in a personal safe. While only you hold the key, nobody else can spend it and no custodian can block it. If the combination is forgotten, nobody can open the safe.

What a custodial balance is

After a deposit, the number shown in a custodial account is an entry in the company’s internal database. The coins sit in wallets that the company controls, often pooled with those of other customers. Trades between customers of one exchange usually change only that database. Nothing is written to the blockchain until someone withdraws.

The customer therefore depends on the company staying solvent, staying secure and honouring withdrawals. This dependence is a form of counterparty risk. Whether customer assets are kept separate, insured or protected by law varies widely between companies and jurisdictions.

Going deeper

“Not your keys, not your coins.” This common saying is accurate about custodial balances: the customer holds a claim, not a key. It leaves out the other half. With self-custody, every failure is the holder’s own, and there is no one to appeal to.

Mixed arrangements. Many people use both models for different purposes. Some wallets also split control between several keys or parties, so that no single device or person can move funds alone. These designs change who must be trusted. They do not remove trust.

What connects to what. A decentralized exchange works with non-custodial wallets: the user signs each action. That removes the exchange as custodian and adds the risks covered in signing transactions.

Custodial does not mean careless. A custodial account still needs protecting. The login is the key in practice, which is why exchange account security has its own page.

Two hypothetical holders on a bad dayHypothetical illustration

Ana holds 100 units of a coin in an account with a company. Ben holds 100 units in a wallet on his phone, with the seed phrase written on one sheet of paper.

Ana’s bad day. The company announces that withdrawals are paused. Her account still shows 100 units, but she cannot move them. Whether and when she gets anything back depends on the company and on the law where it operates.

Ben’s bad day. His phone is stolen and he cannot find the sheet of paper. His 100 units are still on the blockchain, and no company exists that could let him back in.

On an ordinary day, each of them would have said their coins were safe.

What it does NOT mean

  • Custodial does not mean insured. Protection schemes that cover bank deposits generally do not cover crypto balances held with a company.
  • Non-custodial does not mean safe. It removes the company as a risk and makes the holder the only line of defence.
  • A wallet app made by a company is not necessarily custodial. What matters is where the keys are generated and stored, not whose logo is on the app.
  • A balance on a screen is not the same thing in both models. In one it is a claim on a company. In the other it is a reading of the blockchain.
  • Neither model is the right one for everybody. They suit different needs and different abilities to manage a secret.

Common mistakes

  1. Not knowing which kind is in use. If an app never showed a seed phrase and access is by email and password, it is often custodial, although some non-custodial wallets also offer email or social sign-in.
  2. Assuming a company can always return funds. Withdrawals can be delayed, limited or stopped.
  3. Moving to self-custody without a checked backup. A seed phrase that was never written down correctly protects nothing. Check it only with the wallet’s own on-device recovery check or backup screen, or, for a hardware wallet, by restoring onto a genuine spare device that you set up yourself and reset afterwards. Never type the words into a website or a look-alike app.
  4. Treating a custodial login casually. A reused password on an account without a second factor is an open door.

Risks and limitations

  • Custodial: company failure, hacks, frozen withdrawals, account lockouts and changing terms.
  • Non-custodial: lost seed phrases, phishing, malicious signatures and no recovery process.
  • Rules on how custodians must hold customer assets differ between countries and are still developing.
  • This page explains the two models. It does not say where anyone should keep funds.

Key takeaway

Custody is the question of who holds the keys. A custodial balance is a claim on a company, a non-custodial balance is controlled by the holder alone, and each carries risks the other does not.
