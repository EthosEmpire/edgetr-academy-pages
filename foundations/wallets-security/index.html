Wallets & Security

Keys, seed phrases, custody, approvals and how common scams work.

Start here: What Is a Crypto Wallet?

17 pages

What Is a Crypto Wallet? A crypto wallet is software or a device that stores and uses the private keys controlling funds on a blockchain. The coins themselves are records on the blockchain, not files in the wallet. Beginner
Private Keys A private key is a secret number that authorises spending from a blockchain address. Whoever has the key controls the funds, and nobody can reset or reissue it. Beginner
Seed Phrases A seed phrase is a list of words, commonly 12 or 24, from which a wallet can regenerate all of its private keys. It is the master secret and the only backup of the wallet. Beginner
Custodial & Non-Custodial Wallets In a custodial wallet a company holds the private keys for its customers. In a non-custodial wallet the user holds them. The difference decides who can move, freeze or lose the funds. Beginner
Hot & Cold Wallets A hot wallet keeps private keys on an internet-connected device, and a cold wallet keeps them offline. The trade-off is convenience against exposure to remote attacks. Beginner
Hardware Wallets A hardware wallet is a small dedicated device that stores private keys offline and signs transactions on the device itself, so the keys never reach the computer or phone it is used with. Beginner
Addresses & Sending Safely An address is a public identifier, derived from a public key, that funds are sent to. Blockchain transfers cannot be recalled, so the address and the network both have to be right before sending. Beginner
Signing Transactions Signing uses a private key to authorise a transaction or message. The signature is the consent, so a malicious request that gets signed can hand over assets without the seed phrase ever being revealed. Intermediate
Token Approvals A token approval gives a smart contract standing permission to spend a specific token from a wallet, often an unlimited amount, and it stays in force until it is revoked. Intermediate
Phishing Phishing is an attack that imitates a trusted service to trick a person into giving up a secret or signing a harmful request. In crypto the target is usually a seed phrase, a login or a signature. Beginner
Fake Support & Impersonation Fake support is a scam in which someone poses as a help desk, company employee, official or public figure to obtain a seed phrase, a login code, remote access to a device or a payment. Beginner
Fake Airdrops & Wallet Drainers A fake airdrop lures people to a website with the promise of tokens to claim. There a wallet drainer presents signature requests that hand the attacker control of the assets in the wallet. Intermediate
Rug Pulls & Honeypots A rug pull is when a project's developers abandon it and remove the liquidity or funds, leaving holders with a token they cannot sell for value. A honeypot is a token that can be bought but not sold. Intermediate
Pump and Dump A pump and dump is a scheme in which insiders buy an asset cheaply, promote it to drive the price up, and sell to the latecomers the promotion attracted, after which the price collapses. Beginner
Two-Factor Authentication Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds a second proof of identity to a password when signing in. Authenticator apps and hardware security keys are generally stronger second factors than SMS codes. Beginner
Exchange Account Security Exchange account security is the set of protections around a custodial account: a unique password, a strong second factor, a secured email address and, where offered, withdrawal allow-lists and anti-phishing codes. Beginner
Security Checklist A practical checklist of security habits for wallets and exchange accounts: protecting the seed phrase, checking addresses, reading what is signed and recognising the common scams. Beginner
