Derivatives & Perpetuals

Futures, perpetuals, options basics, funding, open interest and mark price.

Start here: What Are Derivatives?

12 pages

What Are Derivatives? A derivative is a contract whose value comes from the price of something else, called the underlying asset, rather than from owning that asset directly. Beginner
Futures Contracts A futures contract is a standardised agreement to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a set future date, traded on an exchange and backed by margin. Intermediate
Perpetual Futures A perpetual future is a futures contract with no expiry date that uses funding payments between longs and shorts to keep its price close to the spot index. Intermediate
Funding Rate The funding rate is a periodic payment exchanged between long and short holders of a perpetual future that pulls the contract price toward the spot index. Intermediate
Open Interest Open interest is the total number, or value, of derivative contracts that are currently open and have not yet been closed or settled. Intermediate
Mark Price & Index Price The index price is an average of spot prices across several exchanges, and the mark price is the reference price built from it that is used for unrealized P&L and liquidation. Intermediate
Margin on Derivatives Margin on derivatives is the collateral posted to open and keep a position: initial margin opens it, and maintenance margin is the minimum needed to keep it open. Intermediate
Liquidation on Perpetuals Liquidation on perpetuals is the forced closing of a leveraged position by the exchange when its margin balance, measured at the mark price, falls to the maintenance margin. Intermediate
Liquidation Cascades A liquidation cascade is a chain reaction in which forced closures of leveraged positions push the price further, which triggers more forced closures. Advanced
Long/Short Ratio The long/short ratio compares long and short positioning in a derivatives market, usually by counting accounts, which is not the same as measuring position size. Advanced
Basis, Contango & Backwardation Basis is the futures price minus the spot price: contango means futures trade above spot, and backwardation means futures trade below spot. Advanced
Options Basics An option is a contract that gives its buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy (call) or sell (put) an asset at a set strike price by a set date. Advanced
