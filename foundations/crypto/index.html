Crypto Basics

Coins, tokens, stablecoins, supply and the vocabulary of crypto.

Start here: What Is Cryptocurrency?

14 pages

What Is Cryptocurrency? A cryptocurrency is a digital asset whose ownership records are kept on a blockchain, a shared ledger run by a network of computers instead of a single bank or company. Beginner
Coins & Tokens A coin is the native asset of its own blockchain, while a token is issued by a smart contract that runs on an existing blockchain and relies on that chain's coin for fees. Beginner
Altcoins Altcoin is a loose label for any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin, covering thousands of assets with very different purposes, designs, liquidity and risks. Beginner
Stablecoins A stablecoin is a token designed to track a reference value, usually one U.S. dollar, using reserves, crypto collateral or an algorithm, each with its own way of failing. Beginner
Ethereum: An Overview Ethereum is a public blockchain, launched in 2015, that runs programs called smart contracts. Its native asset is ether (ETH), which normally pays for each transaction on the network. Beginner
Gas & Network Fees A network fee is what a blockchain charges to process a transaction. On Ethereum-style chains it is measured in gas: the fee is the gas used multiplied by the price per unit of gas. Beginner
Token Supply: Circulating, Total & Max Circulating supply is the number of units in public circulation, total supply is the number that exist now, and max supply is the hard cap, if the asset has one. Beginner
Market Cap & Fully Diluted Valuation Market cap is price multiplied by circulating supply. Fully diluted valuation (FDV) is price multiplied by max or total supply, showing the size implied if every unit existed today. Intermediate
Tokenomics Tokenomics is the economic design of a token: how many units exist, who receives them and when, what the token is used for, and what adds to or removes from its supply. Intermediate
Token Unlocks & Emissions A token unlock releases existing locked tokens to their owners on a schedule, while emissions create new tokens over time as rewards. Both increase the supply that can be traded. Intermediate
Governance & Utility Tokens A governance token carries votes over a protocol's rules or treasury, and a utility token is needed to use a service. Many tokens combine both roles, and neither role is a claim on profits. Intermediate
Meme Coins A meme coin is a crypto asset built around a joke, image or online community instead of a stated function. Its price depends almost entirely on attention and can change violently. Beginner
Airdrops An airdrop is a distribution of tokens to many wallet addresses, usually without payment, often to reward early users or spread ownership. The format is widely imitated by scams. Beginner
A Market That Never Closes Crypto markets trade 24 hours a day, every day of the year, with no opening bell, closing price or weekend break. That changes how candles, liquidity and risk behave. Beginner
