Token Unlocks & Emissions

A token unlock releases existing locked tokens to their owners on a schedule, while emissions create new tokens over time as rewards. Both increase the supply that can be traded.

Why it matters

A token’s supply on launch day is rarely its final supply. Large allocations are usually locked, and new tokens may be created for years. Unlocks and emissions are the two processes that bring that extra supply into the market. They explain why circulating supply grows, why market cap and FDV differ, and why a holder’s share of a token can shrink without any tokens leaving their wallet.

How it works

A token unlock releases tokens that already exist but could not be moved. They were allocated to founders, employees, investors or a treasury, and held under a lock. When the lock ends, the owner can transfer them.

Emissions are new tokens created by the protocol over time. They are paid out as rewards, for example to validators, to people who take part in staking, or to users of an application. Emissions are also called issuance or inflation.

The first moves tokens from locked to circulating. The second increases the total supply itself.

In plain words

An unlock is like a company share plan: the shares were granted years ago, and on a set date the employee is finally allowed to sell them. An emission is like the company printing new shares each month to pay bonuses. Both put more shares into people’s hands.

Vesting schedules

The timetable for unlocks is called a vesting schedule. Its usual parts are:

Term Meaning
Cliff A waiting period during which nothing unlocks. At its end, a first portion is released at once.
Linear vesting After the cliff, the remainder unlocks in equal amounts per month, per day or per block.
Fully vested The point at which the whole allocation is unlocked.
Unlock at launch A portion that is transferable from the first day.

Schedules differ by group. Public allocations are often unlocked at launch, while team and investor allocations commonly have a cliff followed by one to several years of vesting. The details are specific to each project.

Vesting may be enforced by a smart contract, which releases tokens automatically, or by a legal agreement and a custodian. The first can be checked on the blockchain. The second depends on the parties keeping to it.

Emission schedules

Emissions follow a rule, and the rule takes different forms:

  • Fixed and declining. A set amount per block that is cut at intervals. Bitcoin’s issuance, which is reduced at each halving, is the model.
  • A percentage rate. New tokens equal to a stated share of supply each year, sometimes with no end date.
  • Variable. Issuance that adjusts to conditions, such as the share of tokens being staked.
  • Discretionary. Rewards set and changed by a governance vote or a team.

Going deeper

New supply is not the same as selling. An unlock makes tokens transferable. What the recipients do next is their choice. They may hold, stake, sell or use the tokens as collateral. The schedule is public. The behaviour is not.

Why people watch the dates. Unlock calendars are widely followed because of the ordinary logic of supply and demand: extra supply weighs on a market only if it is actually offered for sale and demand does not change, and a calendar shows neither. Because the dates are known in advance, they may also be reflected in the price long beforehand, or not at all. No reliable pattern follows from the date alone.

Size relative to the float. What makes an unlock large is its size compared with the circulating supply, and with how much of the token normally trades. The same number of tokens is a small event for a widely circulated token and a big one for a thinly circulated one.

A cliff and a year of emissionsHypothetical illustration

Hypothetical numbers. A token has 400 million units circulating in month 11. A team and investor allocation of 240 million is locked, with a 12-month cliff releasing 25% and the rest vesting evenly over 36 months.

  • At the cliff: 240M x 25% = 60M unlocks. Circulating supply becomes 400M + 60M = 460M, an increase of 60 / 400 = 15%.
  • Each month after: (240M minus 60M) / 36 = 5M. In month 13 circulating supply is 465M, up 5 / 460 = about 1.1%.
  • After 36 such months: 60M + 36 x 5M = 240M. The allocation is fully vested.

Separately, the protocol emits 2 million new tokens a month as staking rewards. Over 12 months that is 24 million new units, which raise total supply as well as circulating supply.

A schedule is a description, not a forecast

Unlock and emission data show when supply can increase. They do not show whether anyone will sell, or how the market price will respond. Treating a calendar date as a prediction is a common and costly error.

What it does NOT mean

  • An unlock is not a sale. It is permission to transfer. Many unlocked tokens are held or staked.
  • A large unlock does not tell you the direction of price. Known events can be anticipated, ignored or outweighed by other news.
  • “Locked” does not mean unable to affect the market. Locked tokens can sometimes be staked, used for voting or sold through private agreements.
  • Emissions are not free rewards. Tokens paid out as rewards are added to the supply, diluting those who do not receive them.
  • A published schedule is not immutable. Governance or administrators may be able to change it.

Common misunderstandings

  1. Looking only at today’s circulating supply. The schedule shows what that figure is set to become.
  2. Confusing unlocks with emissions. One releases existing tokens. The other creates new ones.
  3. Judging an unlock by token count. Its size relative to the circulating supply is what describes its scale.
  4. Assuming the calendar is accurate. Third-party trackers rely on project disclosures and can be wrong or out of date.

Risks and limits

  • Vesting terms enforced off-chain cannot be verified from public data.
  • Contract administrators may hold powers to mint or release tokens outside the schedule.
  • Supply growth dilutes each existing holder’s share of the total.
  • Tracker data, wallet labels and circulating-supply figures are estimates.

Key takeaway

Unlocks release tokens that already exist, and emissions create new ones. Both enlarge the tradable supply on a schedule that can be studied in advance, while the reaction of holders and of the market cannot.
