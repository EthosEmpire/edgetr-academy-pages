Altcoins

Altcoin is a loose label for any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin, covering thousands of assets with very different purposes, designs, liquidity and risks.

Why it matters

Outside Bitcoin, almost every crypto asset a newcomer meets is called an altcoin. The word sounds like a category, but it is closer to “everything else”. Knowing how loose the label is helps you read market commentary without assuming that thousands of unrelated assets behave as one.

What the word means

Altcoin is short for “alternative coin”: any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin. The term dates from the early years, when most new projects were modified copies of Bitcoin’s software and the name described them well.

Today the label covers assets that have almost nothing in common:

  • native coins of smart-contract blockchains such as Ethereum;
  • stablecoins designed to hold a steady value;
  • tokens tied to a single application;
  • governance tokens that carry votes;
  • meme coins with no stated function.

Usage is not consistent. Some writers leave ether or stablecoins out of “altcoins”, and many use the word for tokens as well as coins. It is a convention of speech, not a technical class.

In plain words

“Altcoin” works like the phrase “foreign food”. It tells you what something is not, and almost nothing about what it is. A bowl of soup and a slab of chocolate both qualify.

How people describe the group

Bitcoin dominance

Bitcoin dominance is Bitcoin’s share of the combined market capitalisation of all cryptocurrencies. It is the most quoted single number about altcoins as a group, because whatever is not Bitcoin’s share belongs to everything else.

Dominance is a ratio, so it moves for several reasons. It can fall because other assets rose faster, because Bitcoin fell further, or simply because new tokens were created and added to the total. Different data sites also count different sets of assets, so their figures do not match exactly.

“Altcoin season”

The phrase “altcoin season” is market slang for a period when many altcoins rise faster than Bitcoin. It has no agreed definition and no reliable start or end. Such periods are identified by looking back, and many assets do not take part in them.

Going deeper

Why so many exist. Launching a new token takes minutes and very little money. Launching a new blockchain is harder but is still open to anyone. The result is a long list in which most entries are small, lightly traded and short-lived.

What tends to differ from Bitcoin. These are tendencies, not laws:

  • Liquidity. Smaller assets usually have thinner markets. Thin liquidity means larger price moves from the same order size and wider gaps between buyers and sellers.
  • Supply design. Many tokens have large allocations to founders and early backers that are released over time. This is the subject of tokenomics.
  • Dependence. A token depends on its own contract, its team, and the chain it runs on.
  • Correlation. Crypto assets have often moved in the same direction at the same time, especially in sharp declines, so holding many of them has not reliably spread risk. See diversification and correlation.

Reading a dominance figureHypothetical illustration

Hypothetical numbers. In the first snapshot, all cryptocurrencies together are valued at 1,000, of which Bitcoin is 550.

  • Bitcoin dominance: 550 / 1,000 = 55%.
  • Everything else: 450.

In the second snapshot, the total is 1,200 and Bitcoin is 600.

  • Bitcoin dominance: 600 / 1,200 = 50%.
  • Everything else: 600.

Bitcoin’s value rose by 50, or about 9%. Everything else rose by 150, or about 33%. Dominance fell even though Bitcoin went up. The figure describes relative size and nothing about any single altcoin: within that 600, some assets may have doubled and others may have gone to zero.

What it does NOT mean

  • “Altcoin” does not mean inferior, and it does not mean improved. It only means “not Bitcoin”.
  • Altcoins are not one market. A stablecoin, a platform coin and a meme coin share a label and little else.
  • Falling dominance is not a forecast. It describes what has already happened to relative sizes.
  • A low unit price does not mean an asset is small or cheap. Size depends on price multiplied by supply. See market cap and FDV.

Common misunderstandings

  1. Treating the group as a single thing. Averages hide the fact that individual outcomes range from large gains to total loss.
  2. Assuming every altcoin follows Bitcoin. Many do move together for long stretches, and then stop doing so without warning.
  3. Comparing unit prices. One unit of two different assets is not a like-for-like quantity.

Risks and limits

  • Many altcoins have short histories, concentrated ownership and thin markets.
  • Projects are abandoned regularly, and a token can stay listed long after development has stopped.
  • Information is uneven and often comes from parties with an interest in the price.
  • Category labels are informal and are applied differently by each data provider.

Key takeaway

“Altcoin” means any cryptocurrency that is not Bitcoin. It is a label of convenience covering assets with unrelated designs and risks, so each one has to be understood on its own terms.
