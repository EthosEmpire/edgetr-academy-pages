Bitcoin Knowledge Center

A neutral technical history: the white paper, mining, the 21 million limit, halvings and nodes.

Start here: What Is Bitcoin?

15 pages

What Is Bitcoin? Bitcoin is a digital currency and payment network that runs without a central operator, using a public ledger that thousands of independent computers verify. Beginner
Satoshi Nakamoto Satoshi Nakamoto is the name used by the unidentified person or group who designed Bitcoin, wrote its white paper and released its first software. Beginner
The Bitcoin White Paper The Bitcoin white paper is the nine-page document from 2008 in which Satoshi Nakamoto described how digital cash could work without a trusted third party. Beginner
Genesis Block & Early History The genesis block is the first block of the Bitcoin blockchain, dated 3 January 2009. This page follows the network from that block through its first few years. Beginner
Proof of Work & Mining Mining is the process by which Bitcoin blocks are created. Miners compete to find a hash below a target, and that costly search is called proof of work. Beginner
The 21 Million Limit Bitcoin's rules allow just under 21 million bitcoin to be created. The limit is the result of a block subsidy that halves every 210,000 blocks until it reaches zero. Beginner
The Halving The halving is the scheduled event, every 210,000 blocks, at which the number of new bitcoin created with each block is cut in half. Beginner
The UTXO Model Bitcoin does not store account balances. It tracks unspent transaction outputs (UTXOs): discrete chunks of bitcoin that each transaction consumes and replaces. Intermediate
Mempool & Confirmations The mempool is where valid Bitcoin transactions wait before a miner includes them in a block. Each block added after that counts as one confirmation. Intermediate
Nodes & Decentralization A Bitcoin node is a computer that independently checks blocks and transactions against the rules. Decentralization describes how widely that checking is spread. Intermediate
Keys & Addresses In Bitcoin, a private key is the secret that authorises spending, a public key is derived from it, and an address is a shareable form used to receive payments. Intermediate
Fees & Block Space Bitcoin blocks have limited room, so transactions compete for it by offering fees. The fee depends on a transaction's data size, not on the amount being sent. Intermediate
The Lightning Network The Lightning Network is a second layer built on Bitcoin in which two parties lock funds in a shared channel and exchange many payments without recording each one on the blockchain. Advanced
Common Bitcoin Misconceptions Many popular statements about Bitcoin, from both enthusiasts and critics, are inaccurate. This page compares common claims with how the system actually works. Beginner
Why Bitcoin Matters Historically Bitcoin was the first working system to let an open network agree on a shared ledger of digital money without a central record keeper, and later projects built on that idea. Beginner
