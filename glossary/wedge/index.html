Glossary term

Wedge Pattern

Also called: rising wedge, falling wedge

A chart pattern in which price narrows between two converging trend lines that both slope in the same direction.

In a rising wedge both lines slope up, with the lower line steeper; in a falling wedge both slope down, with the upper line steeper. Convention classes a rising wedge as bearish and a falling wedge as bullish, because the narrowing swings are read as fading momentum. Wedges are easy to confuse with channels and triangles, and they fail often.

Learn more

Chart Patterns Rising Wedge A rising wedge is price climbing between two upward-sloping lines that converge. Each new high gains less ground than the last, which is conventionally read as weakening momentum. Intermediate▼ Bearish context Chart Patterns Falling Wedge A falling wedge is price declining between two downward-sloping lines that converge. Each new low gains less ground than the last, which is conventionally read as fading selling pressure. Intermediate▲ Bullish context
