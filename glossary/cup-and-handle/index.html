Cup and Handle

Also called: cup-and-handle, cup with handle

A chart pattern made of a rounded, U-shaped decline and recovery (the cup) followed by a smaller pullback (the handle).

It was popularised in stock trading by William O'Neil and is conventionally classed as a bullish continuation pattern, treated as complete on a move above the rim of the cup. The pattern usually takes weeks or months to form. Its shape is judged by eye, so what counts as a valid cup differs between analysts.

Chart Patterns Cup and Handle A cup and handle is a rounded dip and recovery to a prior high, followed by a smaller pullback. It is conventionally read as a pause within an advance, completed if price closes above the rim. Intermediate▲ Bullish context Chart Patterns Rounded Top & Rounded Bottom A rounded top or bottom is a slow, curved change of direction with no sharp turning point. It describes a trend that fades gradually, and it is usually clear only after it has finished. Intermediate
