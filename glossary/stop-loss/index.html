Glossary term

Stop Loss

Also called: stop-loss, stop-loss order, SL

An order placed in advance to close a position if price moves against it to a chosen level, limiting the planned loss.

A stop loss defines, before the trade is opened, the level at which the trade idea is treated as wrong. It is not a fixed exit price: in gaps or fast markets the fill can be worse than the stop level. A stop loss is chosen by the trader, while liquidation is a forced closure by the exchange.

ExampleHypothetical illustration

Hypothetical: a long opened at 100 with a stop loss at 95 has a planned loss of 5 per unit, before fees and slippage.

Trading Mechanics Stop Loss A stop loss is a stop order placed in advance to close a position if price moves against it to a chosen level, limiting the loss approximately but not exactly. Beginner Risk Management Stop Placement Stop placement is the choice of the price at which a trade idea is treated as wrong, and it sets the stop distance that position sizing depends on. Intermediate Compare Stop Loss vs Liquidation A stop loss is the trader's own planned exit order. Liquidation is a forced close by the exchange when margin no longer covers the loss. One is chosen, the other is imposed. Intermediate
